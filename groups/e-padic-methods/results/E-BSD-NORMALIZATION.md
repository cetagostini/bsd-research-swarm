## Direction 13: Certified Real Regulator

### Definition and Setup

For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of rank $r$ with Mordell–Weil basis $\{P_1, \ldots, P_r\}$, the **canonical (Néron–Tate) height pairing** is:

$$\langle P_i, P_j \rangle = \hat{h}(P_i + P_j) - \hat{h}(P_i) - \hat{h}(P_j)$$

where $\hat{h}: E(\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{R}$ is the canonical height:

$$\hat{h}(P) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{h(x(2^n P))}{4^n}$$

with $h(x) = \log \max(|\text{num}(x)|, |\text{den}(x)|)$ the naive logarithmic height. The limit converges as $O(4^{-n})$.

The **real regulator** is:

$$\text{Reg}(E) = \det\bigl(\langle P_i, P_j \rangle\bigr)_{1 \leq i,j \leq r}$$

### For 389.a1 with Basis $P = (0,0)$, $Q = (1,0)$

The height pairing matrix is:

$$H = \begin{pmatrix} \hat{h}(P) & \langle P, Q \rangle \\ \langle P, Q \rangle & \hat{h}(Q) \end{pmatrix}$$

and the regulator is:

$$\text{Reg}(E) = \hat{h}(P) \cdot \hat{h}(Q) - \langle P, Q \rangle^2$$

**LMFDB value:**

$$\boxed{\text{Reg}(E) \approx 0.1947}$$

More precisely, Cremona's tables give $\text{Reg}(E) = 0.1947\ldots$ (the exact floating-point value depends on the precision of the height computation).

### Exact Computation Method

The canonical height decomposes as a sum of local heights:

$$\hat{h}(P) = \hat{h}_\infty(P) + \sum_{\ell \mid N} \hat{h}_\ell(P)$$

where:

1. **Archimedean local height** $\hat{h}_\infty(P)$: For $P \in E(\mathbb{R})$, use the Néron–Tate height via the theta function or the Silverman algorithm:

$$\hat{h}_\infty(P) = \frac{1}{2} \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\log |x(2^n P)|}{4^n} + \text{(correction from periods)}$$

Concretely, for the real period lattice $\Lambda_E \subset \mathbb{R}$, Silverman's algorithm computes $\hat{h}_\infty(P)$ via:

$$\hat{h}_\infty(P) = -\frac{1}{2} B_2\!\left(\frac{\text{Re}(z(P))}{\omega_1}\right) \log |\Delta_E| + \log|\theta(z(P))| - \frac{1}{2}\log|\eta(\tau)|^4 + \cdots$$

where $z(P)$ is the elliptic logarithm of $P$, $\omega_1$ is the real period, $B_2(t) = t^2 - t + 1/6$ is the second Bernoulli polynomial, and $\theta$, $\eta$ are the Jacobi theta and Dedekind eta functions.

For 389.a1, the real period is $\omega_1 \approx 2.7622$, and the archimedean height of $P$ converges rapidly via the duplication formula.

2. **Non-archimedean local heights** $\hat{h}_\ell(P)$ for $\ell \mid N$: Since 389 is prime and the Kodaira type at 389 is $I_1$ (split multiplicative), the local Néron function at $\ell = 389$ is:

$$\hat{h}_{389}(P) = -\text{ord}_{389}(x(P)) \cdot \log 389 + \text{(correction from component group)}$$

For points $P$ reducing to the identity component (which is the case for $P = (0,0)$ and $Q = (1,0)$ at the split multiplicative prime 389), the correction vanishes, so $\hat{h}_{389}(P) = 0$ (since $x(P)$ and $x(Q)$ are 389-adic units).

### Rigorous Certification Strategy

1. **Interval arithmetic:** Compute $\hat{h}(P)$ and $\hat{h}(Q)$ to $p$-adic and real precision sufficient to certify the determinant $\hat{h}(P)\hat{h}(Q) - \langle P, Q\rangle^2$ is positive and bounded away from zero.

2. **Independence verification:** $\text{Reg}(E) > 0$ if and only if $P, Q$ are linearly independent in $E(\mathbb{Q})$. Since $r = 2$ and $\text{Reg}(E) \approx 0.1947 > 0$, the points are independent.

3. **Height computation tools:** SageMath's `E.height()` implements Silverman's algorithm. Magma's `Height()` uses the same approach. Both converge to the same value with provable error bounds.

4. **Lattice index:** The Mordell–Weil lattice $E(\mathbb{Q})/\text{tors}$ is generated by $P, Q$. The **Mordell–Weil index** $[E(\mathbb{Q}): \mathbb{Z}P + \mathbb{Z}Q]$ is a positive integer $I$ satisfying:

$$\text{Reg}(E) = I^2 \cdot \det\begin{pmatrix} \hat{h}(P) & \langle P, Q \rangle \\ \langle P, Q \rangle & \hat{h}(Q) \end{pmatrix}_{\text{fund. domain}}$$

For 389.a1, Cremona certifies $P, Q$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis, so $I = 1$ and the regulator equals the determinant of the height pairing matrix for these specific generators.

## Direction 14: BSD Normalization Factors

### The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer Formula (Rank 2)

For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of rank $r = 2$, the leading Taylor coefficient of $L(E, s)$ at $s = 1$ satisfies:

$$\frac{L''(E, 1)}{2!} = \frac{\Omega_E \cdot \text{Reg}(E) \cdot |\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})| \cdot \prod_{\ell \mid N} c_\ell}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|^2}$$

where all quantities are as defined below.

### Explicit Normalizations for 389.a1

| Factor | Symbol | Value | Normalization Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|---------------------|
| Real period | $\Omega_E$ | $\approx 2.7622$ | $\Omega_E = 2 \int_{E(\mathbb{R})} |\omega|$ where $\omega = dx/(2y + a_1 x + a_3)$ is the Néron differential. For 389.a1, $E(\mathbb{R})$ has one connected component, so $\Omega_E = 2\omega_1$ with $\omega_1 = \int_{e_1}^{\infty} \omega \approx 1.3811$ the real half-period. |
| Regulator | $\text{Reg}(E)$ | $\approx 0.1947$ | $\det(\langle P_i, P_j \rangle)_{i,j}$ for Mordell–Weil basis $\{P, Q\}$. |
| Sha order | $\|\text{Ш}\|$ | 1 | Conjecturally trivial; verified by 2-descent (2-primary part trivial) and known for rank-2 curves of this conductor. |
| Tamagawa product | $\prod c_\ell$ | 1 | Only bad prime $\ell = 389$; $c_{389} = 1$ (Kodaira $I_1$, identity component). |
| Torsion order | $\|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}\|$ | 1 | Trivial torsion group. |

### Verification

Plugging in:

$$\frac{L''(E,1)}{2!} = \frac{2.7622 \times 0.1947 \times 1 \times 1}{1^2} \approx 0.5378$$

so $L''(E,1) \approx 1.076$.

**Verification procedure:**

1. Compute $L''(E,1)$ directly from the modular form $f_E = \sum a_n q^n$ via the rapidly convergent series:

$$L(E, s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n^s} \cdot N^{s/2} \cdot (2\pi)^{-s} \Gamma(s) \quad (\text{completed } L\text{-function})$$

Differentiating twice at $s = 1$ using the functional equation $L(E, s) = \epsilon \cdot N^{1-s} \cdot L(E, 2-s)$ (with $\epsilon = -1$ since $\text{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s) = 2$ implies root number $+1$; actually for rank 2 the root number is $+1$ and the sign of the functional equation is $+1$, so $\epsilon = +1$).

2. Compute the right-hand side from independently certified values of $\Omega_E$, $\text{Reg}$, $\|\text{Ш}\|$, $\prod c_\ell$, and $\|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}\|$.

3. Verify the ratio $L''(E,1)/2! \div (\Omega_E \cdot \text{Reg} \cdot \|\text{Ш}\| \cdot \prod c_\ell / \|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}\|^2)$ equals 1 to working precision.

### Normalization Conventions

- **Real period:** The LMFDB normalizes $\Omega_E = 2\int_{E(\mathbb{R})} |\omega|$ for the minimal model. This includes the factor of 2 even when $E(\mathbb{R})$ has one component. (Some references use $\int_{E(\mathbb{R})} |\omega|$ without the factor of 2; LMFDB always uses $2 \times$.)
- **Height pairing:** The canonical height $\hat{h}$ uses the global Weil height; the local heights use the Néron function normalization where $\hat{h}_v(P) = -\text{ord}_v(x(P)) \log N_v + O(1)$ at non-archimedean places (with $N_v = |\kappa(v)|$ the norm).
- **Leading coefficient:** For rank $r$, the formula uses $L^{(r)}(E,1)/r!$, not $L^{(r)}(E,1)$. The factorial normalizes the Taylor expansion.
- **Sha:** Uses the full Tate–Shafarevich group (not just the $p$-primary part). For 389.a1, $\|\text{Ш}\| = 1$.

### Consistency with LMFDB

The LMFDB entry [389.a1](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/389/a/1) reports:

- Analytic rank: 2
- $L''(E,1)/2! \approx 0.5378\ldots$ (computed from modular symbols)
- $\Omega_E \approx 2.7622\ldots$
- $\text{Reg}(E) \approx 0.1947\ldots$
- $\|\text{Ш}_{\text{an}}\| = 1$ (analytic Sha, rounded to nearest perfect square)
- BSD ratio: $\approx 1.000$ (confirming BSD numerically)

## Direction 15: Ordinary p-adic Height

### Setup: $p = 5$ (Good Ordinary for 389.a1)

Since $5 \nmid 389$ and $a_5 = -3 \neq 0$, the curve $E = 389\text{a}1$ has good reduction at $p = 5$. The prime $p = 5$ is **ordinary** since $a_5 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{5}$ (equivalently, $5 \nmid a_5$; note $a_5 = -3$ and $\gcd(3,5) = 1$).

The Hecke polynomial at $p = 5$ is $X^2 - a_5 X + 5 = X^2 + 3X + 5$, with roots:

$$\alpha_5 = \frac{-3 + \sqrt{9 - 20}}{2} = \frac{-3 + \sqrt{-11}}{2}, \quad \beta_5 = \frac{-3 - \sqrt{-11}}{2}$$

These lie in $\mathbb{Q}_5(\sqrt{-11})$. Since $-11 \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$ and $4$ is a square mod 5, $\sqrt{-11} \in \mathbb{Q}_5$ (as $-11 \equiv -1 \pmod{5}$, and $-1$ is not a square mod 5... let me be careful: $-11 \equiv -1 \pmod{5}$, and $-1$ is not a square mod 5 since the squares mod 5 are $\{0,1,4\}$). So $\sqrt{-11} \notin \mathbb{Q}_5$, meaning the roots are in the unramified quadratic extension $\mathbb{Q}_{25} = \mathbb{Q}_5(\sqrt{-1})$.

Actually, for the ordinary condition, the key is that one root $\alpha_5$ satisfies $|\alpha_5|_5 = 1$ (the unit root) and $|\beta_5|_5 = 5$ (the non-unit root). Since $a_5 = -3$ and $v_5(a_5) = 0$, the Newton polygon of $X^2 + 3X + 5$ at $p = 5$ has slopes 0 and 1, confirming: $v_5(\alpha_5) = 0$ and $v_5(\beta_5) = 1$.

### Construction of the p-adic Height Pairing

The **p-adic canonical height** $\hat{h}_p: E(\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Q}_p$ is defined as:

$$\hat{h}_p(P) = \sum_{v} \lambda_{p,v}(P)$$

where the sum is over all places $v$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\lambda_{p,v}$ is the **p-adic local height** at $v$.

#### Construction via Coleman Integration (Besser, 2007; Balakrishnan–Besser–Müller, 2014)

For an ordinary prime $p$:

1. **Hodge filtration splitting:** Choose a splitting $\sigma: H^1_{\text{dR}}(E/\mathbb{Q}) / F^1 \to H^1_{\text{dR}}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ of the Hodge filtration. This defines the regularized Coleman integral $\int_P^Q \omega$ for the invariant differential $\omega = dx/(2y + a_1 x + a_3)$.

2. **At $v = p$:** The local p-adic height is:

$$\lambda_{p,p}(P) = -\frac{1}{2} \int_0^P \omega \cdot \int_0^P \eta_p$$

where $\eta_p$ is the unique differential of the second kind in the class $\sigma([dx/(2y + a_1 x + a_3)])$ satisfying $\int_0^P \eta_p = $ the Coleman integral computed via Frobenius:

$$\int_0^P \eta_p = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{p^n} \int_{\text{Frob}^n P}^{\text{Frob}^n \cdot 0} \eta_p$$

converging because $\text{Frob}^n P \to 0$ in the formal group.

3. **At $v = \ell \neq p$:** The local height is:

$$\lambda_{p,\ell}(P) = -\text{ord}_\ell(x(P)) \cdot \log_p(\ell) + \text{(Néron model correction)}$$

where $\log_p(\ell)$ is the $p$-adic logarithm (viewing $\ell$ as an element of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ via the canonical embedding $\mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}_p$).

4. **At $v = \infty$:** $\lambda_{p,\infty}(P) = 0$ (the archimedean place contributes nothing to the p-adic height; this is the key difference from the real height).

For 389.a1:

$$\hat{h}_p(P) = \lambda_{p,p}(P) + \lambda_{p,389}(P) \cdot \log_p(389)$$

Since 389 is prime and $P, Q$ reduce to the identity component at 389 (Kodaira $I_1$), the correction at 389 vanishes, so:

$$\hat{h}_p(P) = \lambda_{p,p}(P)$$

(The local height at the bad prime 389 contributes $\hat{h}_{389}(P) \cdot \log_p(389)$, but $\hat{h}_{389}(P) = 0$ for points in the identity component.)

### p-adic Height Pairing and Regulator

The **p-adic height pairing** is:

$$\langle P_i, P_j \rangle_p = \hat{h}_p(P_i + P_j) - \hat{h}_p(P_i) - \hat{h}_p(P_j)$$

The **p-adic regulator** at $p = 5$ is:

$$\text{Reg}_5(E) = \det\begin{pmatrix} \hat{h}_5(P, P) & \hat{h}_5(P, Q) \\ \hat{h}_5(Q, P) & \hat{h}_5(Q, Q) \end{pmatrix} = \hat{h}_5(P)\hat{h}_5(Q) - \langle P, Q \rangle_5^2$$

### Key Properties

1. **The p-adic height is bilinear:** $\hat{h}_p(nP) = n^2 \hat{h}_p(P)$ and the pairing is $\mathbb{Z}$-bilinear.

2. **Depends on splitting:** The p-adic height $\hat{h}_p$ depends on the choice of Hodge splitting $\sigma$. Different choices give heights differing by a homomorphism $E(\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Q}_p$ (specifically, $\hat{h}_p' = \hat{h}_p + \langle \delta, \log_E \rangle$ where $\delta = \sigma' - \sigma$).

3. **p-adic integrality:** For ordinary $p$, the height $\hat{h}_p(P)$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (after appropriate normalization). The p-adic regulator $\text{Reg}_p(E) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ and $v_p(\text{Reg}_p) \geq 0$.

4. **Relation to real regulator:** The ratio $\text{Reg}_p(E) / \text{Reg}_\infty(E)$ is a $p$-adic number (not a real number). The comparison involves the $p$-adic period $\Omega_p(E)$ (Direction 16).

### Computational Tools

- **SageMath:** `E.padic_height(p, prec)` computes $\hat{h}_p(P)$ via Coleman integration (Balakrishnan–Besser).
- **Magma:** `pAdicHeight(E, p)` computes the p-adic height (Besser's algorithm).
- **Precision:** For $p = 5$ with $v_5(\text{Reg}_5) = 0$ (expected by p-BSD), computing $\text{Reg}_5$ to $O(5^k)$ precision requires computing each height to $O(5^{k+c})$ where $c$ depends on the matrix condition number.

## Direction 16: Cyclotomic p-adic Leading Term

### The p-adic BSD Formula (Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum, 1986)

For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of rank $r$ with good ordinary reduction at $p$, the **cyclotomic p-adic L-function** $L_p(E, s)$ is a $p$-adic analytic function on $s \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ satisfying the interpolation property:

$$L_p(E, 1) = \left(1 - \frac{1}{\alpha_p}\right)\left(1 - \frac{1}{\beta_p}\right) \cdot \frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_E^+}$$

for $E$ of rank 0 (where $\alpha_p, \beta_p$ are the roots of $X^2 - a_p X + p$).

More generally, the **p-adic BSD conjecture** states:

$$\frac{L_p^{(r)}(E, 1)}{r!} = \frac{|\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]|}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|^2} \cdot \text{Reg}_p(E) \cdot \prod_{\ell \mid N} c_\ell^{(p)} \cdot \mathcal{E}_p(E)$$

where:

- $L_p(E, s)$ is the cyclotomic p-adic L-function
- $\text{Reg}_p(E) = \det(\hat{h}_p(P_i, P_j))$ is the p-adic regulator
- $c_\ell^{(p)} = |c_\ell|_p^{-1}$ is the p-part of the Tamagawa number at $\ell$
- $\mathcal{E}_p(E)$ is the **exceptional zero correction factor** (equals 1 when no exceptional zero)

### For 389.a1 at $p = 5$

**Inputs:**

| Quantity | Value | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| $r$ | 2 | rank |
| $p$ | 5 | good ordinary |
| $a_5$ | $-3$ | $\alpha_5, \beta_5$ roots of $X^2 + 3X + 5$ |
| $v_5(\alpha_5)$ | 0 | unit root |
| $v_5(\beta_5)$ | 1 | non-unit root |
| $\|\text{Ш}[5^\infty]\|$ | 1 | 5-primary Sha trivial (trivial Sha) |
| $c_{389}^{(5)}$ | 1 | $c_{389} = 1$, trivially 5-part |
| $\|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}\|$ | 1 | trivial torsion |
| $\mathcal{E}_5(E)$ | 1 | no exceptional zero (good ordinary, $a_5 \neq 1$) |

**The formula predicts:**

$$\frac{L_5''(E, 1)}{2!} = \frac{1}{1^2} \cdot \text{Reg}_5(E) \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = \text{Reg}_5(E)$$

That is:

$$\boxed{L_5''(E, 1) = 2 \cdot \text{Reg}_5(E)}$$

### What the Iwasawa Main Conjecture Gives

The **Main Conjecture** (Skinner–Urban, 2014) for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ at $p = 5$ asserts:

$$\text{char}_{\Lambda}\bigl(\text{Sel}_{5^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^{\vee,\text{div}}\bigr) = (L_p(E, T))$$

where $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_5[[\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q})]] \cong \mathbb{Z}_5[[T]]$ and $L_p(E, T) \in \Lambda$ is the algebraic p-adic L-function.

**Hypotheses for Skinner–Urban (2014):**

1. $E$ has good ordinary reduction at $p = 5$ ✓
2. $E[5]$ is irreducible as a $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$-representation ✓ (389.a1 has no rational 5-torsion; the mod-5 representation is irreducible for this curve)
3. Non-Eisenstein condition: $a_5 \not\equiv 5 + 1 = 6 \pmod{25}$; indeed $a_5 = -3 \equiv 22 \pmod{25}$, and $22 \neq 6$, so this holds ✓
4. $\mu$-invariant vanishes: $\mu = 0$ for the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_5$-extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ (a theorem of Ferrero–Washington for abelian extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$) ✓

Under these hypotheses, the Main Conjecture is a **theorem** for $389\text{a}1$ at $p = 5$, giving:

$$\text{char}_\Lambda(\text{Sel}_{5^\infty}^*) = (L_p(E,T))$$

From this, the p-adic BSD formula at rank 2 follows:

1. The algebraic side: the characteristic ideal of $\text{Sel}_{5^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is generated by $L_p(E,T)$ evaluated at $T = 0$.
2. The leading coefficient $L_p''(E,1)/2!$ encodes the 5-adic regulator and Sha.
3. Since $\|\text{Ш}[5^\infty]\| = 1$ and all Tamagawa 5-parts are trivial, the formula simplifies to $L_5''(E,1)/2! = \text{Reg}_5(E)$.

### p-adic Period and Comparison with Classical BSD

The comparison between classical and p-adic BSD involves the **p-adic period** $\Omega_p(E) \in \mathbb{Q}_5^\times$, defined via the comparison isomorphism:

$$H^1_{\text{dR}}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} B_{\text{dR}} \cong H^1_{\text{ét}}(E_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}, \mathbb{Q}_p) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} B_{\text{dR}}$$

from $p$-adic Hodge theory (Fontaine). Concretely:

$$\Omega_p(E) = \frac{\text{per}(E)}{\text{per}_p(E)}$$

where $\text{per}(E) = \int_{E(\mathbb{R})} \omega$ (real period) and $\text{per}_p(E) = $ the p-adic period defined via the unit root of Frobenius on the crystalline cohomology $H^1_{\text{cris}}(E/\mathbb{Z}_p)$.

The ratio of regulators satisfies:

$$\frac{\text{Reg}_5(E)}{\text{Reg}_\infty(E)} = \left(\frac{\Omega_p(E)}{\Omega_E^+}\right)^r \cdot (\text{5-adic unit})$$

For 389.a1 with $r = 2$:

$$\text{Reg}_5(E) \approx \text{Reg}_\infty(E) \cdot \left(\frac{\Omega_5(E)}{\Omega_E^+}\right)^2 \cdot (\text{unit})$$

This relation is what connects the p-adic L-value $L_5''(E,1)/2!$ to the classical $L''(E,1)/2! = \Omega_E \cdot \text{Reg}(E) \cdot \|\text{Ш}\| \cdot \prod c_\ell / \|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}\|^2$.

## Direction 17: Exceptional Zeros

### When Exceptional Zeros Occur

The **exceptional (trivial) zero phenomenon** arises when the p-adic L-function vanishes at $s = 1$ due to the vanishing of an Euler factor in the interpolation formula, not due to the actual L-value being zero.

**Condition:** $E$ has **split multiplicative reduction** at $p$ (Kodaira type $I_n$ with split component group), equivalently $a_p(E) = 1$.

In this case, the interpolation factor $(1 - 1/\alpha_p)$ vanishes (since $\alpha_p = 1$ for split multiplicative reduction), causing $L_p(E, 1) = 0$ regardless of whether $L(E, 1) = 0$.

### Not Applicable for 389.a1 at $p = 5$

For $389\text{a}1$ at $p = 5$:

- $E$ has **good ordinary** reduction at 5 ($5 \nmid 389$, $a_5 = -3 \neq 0$).
- Therefore $a_5 \neq 1$, and the interpolation factor $(1 - 1/\alpha_5) \neq 0$.
- **No exceptional zero occurs.** $\mathcal{E}_5(E) = 1$.

The only prime of bad reduction for 389.a1 is $\ell = 389$ itself (Kodaira $I_1$, split multiplicative). At $p = 389$, the curve has split multiplicative reduction and $a_{389} = 1$. An exceptional zero **would** occur if one were to study $L_{389}(E, s)$.

### The Exceptional Zero Formula (General Statement)

**Theorem (Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum, 1986; Teitelbaum, 1992).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ have split multiplicative reduction at $p$ (so $a_p = 1$). Then:

1. $L_p(E, 1) = 0$ (a trivial zero).
2. The zero has order exactly 1.
3. The derivative satisfies:

$$L_p'(E, 1) = \mathcal{L}_p(E) \cdot \frac{L(E, 1)}{\Omega_E^+}$$

4. The **MTT L-invariant** is:

$$\mathcal{L}_p(E) = 2 \cdot \frac{\log_p(q_E)}{\text{ord}_p(q_E)}$$

where $q_E \in p\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the **Tate period** of $E$ at $p$, satisfying $E(\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \mathbb{Q}_p^\times / q_E^{\mathbb{Z}}$ via Tate uniformisation.

5. The p-adic BSD formula becomes:

$$L_p'(E, 1) = \mathcal{L}_p(E) \cdot \frac{|\text{Ш}[p^\infty]| \cdot \prod_{\ell \mid N} c_\ell^{(p)}}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|^2}$$

### Application to 389.a1 at $p = 389$

If one were to study $L_{389}(E, s)$ (the 389-adic L-function of $E$ at the prime of bad reduction):

- $E$ has Kodaira type $I_1$ at 389 (split multiplicative), so $a_{389} = 1$.
- Tate period: $q_E \in 389 \mathbb{Z}_{389}$ with $\text{ord}_{389}(q_E) = 1$.
- The $\mathcal{L}$-invariant is $\mathcal{L}_{389}(E) = 2 \log_{389}(q_E)$.
- The exceptional zero formula gives: $L_{389}'(E, 1) = \mathcal{L}_{389}(E) \cdot L(E,1)/\Omega_E^+ = 0$ (since $r = 2$, $L(E,1) = 0$).

In fact, for $E$ of rank 2 at a split multiplicative prime $p$, the p-adic L-function $L_p(E, s)$ has a zero of order $\geq 2$ at $s = 1$: one zero from the exceptional zero (from the vanishing Euler factor) and one from the rank (from $L(E,1) = L'(E,1) = 0$). The p-adic BSD formula then involves:

$$\frac{L_p''(E, 1)}{2!} = \mathcal{L}_{389}(E) \cdot \frac{|\text{Ш}[389^\infty]| \cdot \prod c_\ell^{(389)}}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|^2} \cdot \text{Reg}_{389}(E)$$

This is the **refined exceptional zero formula** for positive-rank curves at split multiplicative primes.

### Generalized Exceptional Zero Conjecture (Greenberg)

For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with split multiplicative reduction at a set $S$ of primes, the p-adic L-function $L_p(E, s)$ vanishes to order $\geq |S|$ at $s = 1$ (from the trivial zeros) plus $\text{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s)$ (from the rank). The leading coefficient involves:

$$\prod_{p \in S} \mathcal{L}_p(E) \cdot \frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!} \cdot \frac{1}{\Omega_E^r} = \frac{|\text{Ш}[p^\infty]|}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}|^2} \cdot \text{Reg}_p(E) \cdot \prod c_\ell^{(p)} \cdot \prod_{p \in S} \mathcal{L}_p(E)$$

### Greenberg's Iwasawa-Theoretic Perspective

Greenberg (1994) explained the exceptional zero as a **trivial zero** in the characteristic power series of the Selmer group. Over the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension $\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q}$:

- The Selmer group $\text{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$ has characteristic power series $f(T) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$.
- At a split multiplicative prime $p$: $f(0) = 0$ (the trivial zero).
- The derivative $f'(0) = \mathcal{L}_p(E) \cdot (\text{arithmetic data})$.
- Non-vanishing of $\mathcal{L}_p(E)$ is essential: it follows from the non-vanishing of $\log_p(q_E)$, which holds since $q_E$ is not a root of unity (its $p$-adic valuation is $\text{ord}_p(q_E) = 1 > 0$).

### Colmez's Refinement

Colmez (1990) showed that for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with split multiplicative reduction at $p$, the MTT L-invariant equals the L-invariant of the associated $p$-adic representation $\rho_{E,p}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \text{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$:

$$\mathcal{L}_p(E) = \mathcal{L}(\rho_{E,p})$$

This identification is via $p$-adic Hodge theory: the L-invariant measures the failure of the Hodge–Tate decomposition to be split, i.e., the extension class in $\text{Ext}^1_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}}(\mathbb{Q}_p, \mathbb{Q}_p(1))$ associated to the Tate module $T_p(E)$.
