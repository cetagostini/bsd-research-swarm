## G-001 Deep Analysis: Taylor-Wiles Patching at Higher Rank

### 1. Extending Taylor-Wiles to Rank 2 (Corank 2 Selmer Groups)

#### Classical Setup (GL₂, Corank 1)

The original Taylor-Wiles method controls $\mathrm{Sel}_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1))$ when this group has $\mathcal{O}$-corank 1. The argument proceeds:

1. Choose $N$ Taylor-Wiles primes $Q = \{q_1, \ldots, q_N\}$ with $q_i \equiv 1 \pmod{\ell^M}$ and $\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_{q_i})$ having distinct eigenvalues $\{\alpha_i, \beta_i\}$.
2. Construct the augmented deformation ring $R_Q$ and the patched ring $R_\infty = R_{Q_\infty}$ as a power series ring over $\mathcal{O}$ in $g = \dim H^1_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ variables, modulo relations.
3. The diamond operators $S_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[x_1, \ldots, x_r]]$ act on the patched module $M_\infty$, where $r = \dim H^1_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1))^\vee$ by Poitou-Tate.
4. **Key freeness**: $M_\infty$ is free of rank 1 over $S_\infty$. This gives $R_\infty \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}_\infty$ and the numerical criterion $\#H^1_\Sigma / H^1_{\Sigma,\mathrm{str}} = \#\Phi$.

The corank-1 hypothesis enters precisely at step 4: freeness over $S_\infty$ is deduced from the fact that $M_\infty$ is a finite-rank $\mathcal{O}$-module whose $S_\infty$-rank equals the corank of the Selmer group, which is 1.

#### Rank 2: What Changes

For corank 2 (i.e., $\mathrm{corank}_\mathcal{O}\, \mathrm{Sel}_\Sigma = 2$), the patched module $M_\infty$ has $S_\infty$-rank 2 instead of 1. The Taylor-Wiles argument changes in three essential ways:

**Step 1: Choice of TW primes.** The number of TW primes must be chosen so that $r = \dim H^1_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1))^\vee = \dim H^1_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) - \dim H^1_{\Sigma,\mathrm{str}}(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) + \dim H^0(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1))$ suffices. For corank 2, we need $r \geq 2$, which forces $N \geq 2$ TW primes. The local-global Euler characteristic gives:

$$\dim H^1_\Sigma - \dim H^1_{\Sigma,\mathrm{str}} = \dim H^0(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1)) + [F:\mathbb{Q}] \cdot \frac{n(n-1)}{2} - \dim H^0(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$$

For $n = 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ this equals 1 generically. For $n = 3$ or for $F \neq \mathbb{Q}$, it grows, and the corank can be 2 or larger.

**Step 2: Freeness over $S_\infty$.** The patched module $M_\infty$ now has rank 2 over $S_\infty$. The argument that $M_\infty$ is *free* over $S_\infty$ requires:

$$M_\infty \cong S_\infty^{\oplus 2}$$

This is the critical change. In the corank-1 case, $M_\infty$ being a finitely generated module over a local ring with $\mathrm{depth}(M_\infty) = \dim(S_\infty)$ forces freeness when the rank is 1 (by the Auslander-Buchsbaum theorem). For rank 2, depth alone does not force freeness — $M_\infty$ could be a nontrivial extension of free modules, i.e., have a presentation matrix with nontrivial entries in $\mathfrak{m}_{S_\infty}$.

**Step 3: The $R = \mathbb{T}$ isomorphism.** Even without full freeness, one can attempt to prove $R_\infty \cong \mathbb{T}_\infty$ as $S_\infty$-algebras. The surjection $R_\infty \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}_\infty$ always exists (from the universal property of deformation rings). The key input is:

$$\mathrm{length}_{S_\infty}(M_\infty / \mathfrak{m}_{S_\infty} M_\infty) = \mathrm{length}_{S_\infty}(S_\infty^{\oplus 2} / \mathfrak{m}_{S_\infty} S_\infty^{\oplus 2}) = 2$$

If $M_\infty / \mathfrak{m}_{S_\infty} M_\infty$ has $\mathcal{O}$-length 2, then $\mathrm{Fitt}_0^{S_\infty}(M_\infty) = 0$ and $R_\infty$ has the correct Krull dimension. But to promote this to an isomorphism, one needs control of the higher Fitting ideals — specifically $\mathrm{Fitt}_1^{S_\infty}(M_\infty) = 0$ — which is equivalent to $M_\infty$ being free.

#### Summary of the Patching Argument for Corank 2

| Component | Corank 1 | Corank 2 |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| $\mathrm{rank}_{S_\infty}(M_\infty)$ | 1 | 2 |
| Freeness proof | Depth + rank 1 | **Fails** — need derived methods |
| $R = \mathbb{T}$ | Follows from freeness | Requires $\mathrm{Fitt}_1 = 0$ or derived patching |
| TW primes needed | $N \geq 1$ | $N \geq 2$ |
| Numerical criterion | $\#H^1_\Sigma/H^1_{\Sigma,\mathrm{str}} = \#\Phi$ | Generalized to Fitting ideal equality |

### 2. The Obstruction: Loss of Freeness over $S_\infty$

#### The Problem Precisely

Let $S_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[x_1, \ldots, x_r]]$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_S = (\ell, x_1, \ldots, x_r)$. The patched module $M_\infty$ is a finitely generated $S_\infty$-module with:

$$M_\infty / \mathfrak{m}_S M_\infty \cong H^1_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) \quad (\text{as } k\text{-vector space})$$

For corank 1, $\dim_k(M_\infty / \mathfrak{m}_S M_\infty) = 1$ implies $M_\infty$ is generated by 1 element, hence $M_\infty \cong S_\infty / I$ for some ideal $I$, and the patching surjection $R_\infty \twoheadrightarrow S_\infty / I \cong \mathbb{T}_\infty$ yields $R_\infty \cong \mathbb{T}_\infty$ when $I = 0$.

For corank 2, $\dim_k(M_\infty / \mathfrak{m}_S M_\infty) = 2$, so $M_\infty$ is generated by 2 elements. We have a surjection:

$$S_\infty^{\oplus 2} \twoheadrightarrow M_\infty$$

with kernel $K$. The obstruction to freeness is exactly the module $K$. The obstruction lives in:

$$\mathrm{Ext}^1_{S_\infty}(M_\infty, S_\infty) \cong H^1(G_{F,\Sigma}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)$$

and the primary source is:

$$H^2(G_{F,\Sigma}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho) \neq 0$$

This group vanishes for $n = 2$ under standard hypotheses (Fontaine-Laffaille, $H^2 = 0$ by the global Euler characteristic and local conditions). For $n \geq 3$, it can be nonzero:

- The local condition at $p$ for GL$_n$ involves the Fontaine-Laffaille module, which has $\dim H^2_\ell(\mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho) = \binom{n}{2} - 1 > 0$ for $n \geq 3$.
- The global Euler characteristic gives $\chi = [F:\mathbb{Q}] \cdot \frac{n(n-1)}{2} - \dim H^0 + \dim H^0(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1))$, which is positive for $n \geq 3$.

#### What Replaces Freeness

When $M_\infty$ is not free over $S_\infty$, three alternatives exist:

**(A) Perfect complex.** Replace $M_\infty$ by a perfect complex $C^\bullet$ of $S_\infty$-modules. The derived Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}^{\mathrm{der}}$ acts on $C^\bullet$ in the derived category. The $R = \mathbb{T}$ isomorphism becomes:

$$R_\infty \cong \mathbb{T}_\infty^{\mathrm{der}} := \mathrm{End}_{D(S_\infty)}(C^\bullet)^{\mathrm{op}}$$

This is the Calegari-Geraghty approach. The Selmer group is recovered as:

$$\mathrm{Sel}_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1)) \cong H^{-1}(C^\bullet \otimes^\mathbb{L}_{S_\infty} k)$$

**(B) Virtual freeness.** $M_\infty$ has a finite free resolution of length $\leq d = \dim S_\infty$:

$$0 \to F_d \to \cdots \to F_1 \to F_0 \to M_\infty \to 0$$

The alternating sum $\sum (-1)^i [F_i]$ in the Grothendieck group $K_0(S_\infty)$ is a well-defined "virtual rank" class. If this class equals $2 \cdot [S_\infty]$, then $M_\infty$ is *virtually free of rank 2*.

**(C) Fitting ideal control.** Even without freeness, the Fitting ideals $\mathrm{Fitt}_j^{S_\infty}(M_\infty)$ for $j = 0, 1, \ldots$ control the structure. The key equality:

$$\mathrm{Fitt}_0^{S_\infty}(M_\infty) = \mathrm{Fitt}_0^{S_\infty}(S_\infty^{\oplus 2}) = 0$$

is automatic. The obstruction is:

$$\mathrm{Fitt}_1^{S_\infty}(M_\infty) \supseteq 0$$

Equality holds iff $M_\infty$ is free. For corank 2, $\mathrm{Fitt}_1(M_\infty) = 0$ is the necessary and sufficient condition for the Selmer group to have the predicted corank.

### 3. The Calegari-Geraghty Fix: Derived Hecke Algebras

#### The Derived Patching Framework

Calegari-Geraghty (2016) resolves the freeness obstruction by replacing modules by complexes throughout. The key construction:

1. **Derived completed cohomology.** Define:
$$\widetilde{H}^i = \varprojproj_{K} H^i(Y_K, \mathcal{O}/\ell^n)$$
where $K$ ranges over compact open subgroups. This is a module over the completed Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}^{\mathrm{der}}$.

2. **Derived Hecke action.** For each Taylor-Wiles prime $q_i$, the local Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}_{q_i}$ acts on $\widetilde{H}^\bullet$. The derived Hecke algebra is:
$$\mathbb{T}_Q^{\mathrm{der}} = \bigotimes_{q \in Q}^{\mathbb{L}} \mathbb{T}_q^{\mathrm{der}}$$

3. **Derived patching.** Construct a perfect complex $C_\infty^\bullet$ of $R_\infty[[S_\infty]]$-modules, equipped with a $\mathbb{T}_\infty^{\mathrm{der}}$-action, such that:
$$R_\infty \cong \mathbb{T}_\infty^{\mathrm{der}}$$
as $S_\infty$-algebras in the homotopy category.

4. **Selmer group extraction.** The Selmer group is:
$$\mathrm{Sel}_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1)) \cong H^0(C_\infty^\bullet \otimes^\mathbb{L}_{S_\infty} S_\infty / \mathfrak{m}_S)$$

#### What's Needed for Rank ≥ 2

The Calegari-Geraghty method handles arbitrary corank in principle, but the implementation requires:

**Requirement 1: $t$-exactness of the completed cohomology functor.** The functor $K \mapsto \widetilde{H}^\bullet(Y_K, -)$ must be $t$-exact with respect to a suitable $t$-structure on the category of Hecke modules. For regular weight, this follows from the vanishing of $\widetilde{H}^i$ for $i \neq d$ (Scholze's vanishing theorem via perfectoid methods). For irregular weight, the cohomology can spread across multiple degrees, destroying $t$-exactness.

**Requirement 2: Smoothness of local deformation rings.** At primes $q \in Q$, the local deformation ring $R_q^{\mathrm{loc}}$ must be smooth over $\mathcal{O}[[S_q]]$. For GL$_n$, this holds when $\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_q}}$ is Fontaine-Laffaille with distinct eigenvalues. For $n = 2$, this is automatic; for $n \geq 3$, the distinctness condition on eigenvalues becomes $n(n-1)/2$ conditions instead of 1.

**Requirement 3: Control of cohomological amplitude.** The complex $C_\infty^\bullet$ must be supported in a known range of degrees. For $n = 2$, the range is $[d-1, d]$ (where $d$ is the cohomological dimension). For $n \geq 3$, the range is $[d-\lfloor n/2 \rfloor, d]$, which requires controlling the cohomology of arithmetic groups in higher degrees.

**Requirement 4: The derived $R = \mathbb{T}$ isomorphism must be compatible with specialization.** The isomorphism $R_\infty \cong \mathbb{T}_\infty^{\mathrm{der}}$ in the derived category must specialize to an isomorphism of rings after taking $H^0$. This requires a "convergence" result for the derived Hecke algebra that is not yet proved in full generality.

#### Assessment for $n = 2$ Corank 2

For $n = 2$ with corank 2 (i.e., the Selmer group has $\mathcal{O}$-corank 2), the Calegari-Geraghty method is expected to work with the following modifications:

- Use **two** Taylor-Wiles primes (so $r = 2$ and $S_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[x_1, x_2]]$).
- The complex $C_\infty^\bullet$ is supported in degrees $[d-1, d]$, so $H^0(C_\infty^\bullet \otimes^\mathbb{L} k)$ is a 2-dimensional $k$-vector space.
- The Fitting ideal $\mathrm{Fitt}_1^{S_\infty}(H^0(C_\infty^\bullet))$ is the "congruence module" whose $\mathcal{O}$-adic valuation equals $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1} L(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho, s)$.

The derived method handles this case completely, provided the $t$-exactness and local smoothness conditions are verified. For GL$_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ with corank 2, these conditions are known to hold under standard hypotheses.

### 4. Computational Test: Deformation Ring Structure for Rank 2 Candidates

#### Setup

Consider $\bar\rho: G_\mathbb{Q} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_7)$ with $\mathrm{Sel}_\Sigma(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1))$ of corank 2. The deformation ring $R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}}$ pro-represents the functor of deformations of $\bar\rho$ with fixed determinant and local conditions $\Sigma$.

#### Predicted Structure

The deformation ring $R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}}$ is a complete local $\mathcal{O}$-algebra with residue field $k = \mathbb{F}_7$. Its structure depends on:

**Dimension count.** The expected Krull dimension is:
$$\dim R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}} = 1 + \dim H^1_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) - \dim H^1_{\Sigma,\mathrm{str}}(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) + \dim H^0(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$$

For corank 2, this is:
$$\dim R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}} = 1 + g = 1 + (r + \dim H^1_{\Sigma,\mathrm{str}} - \dim H^0)$$

where $g$ is the number of generators minus relations.

**Concrete prediction.** For a rank 2 candidate (GL$_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, $\ell = 7$, corank 2):

| Component | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| $\dim H^1_\Sigma(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ | $g \geq 3$ |
| $\dim H^1_{\Sigma,\mathrm{str}}(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ | $g - 2$ |
| $\dim H^0(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ | 1 (scalar matrices) |
| $r = \mathrm{corank}$ | 2 |
| Expected $\dim R_\Sigma$ | $1 + g$ |
| Generators of $R_\Sigma$ | $g$ (over $\mathcal{O}$) |
| Relations | $g - 2$ |

The ring $R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}}$ is a quotient:
$$R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}} \cong \mathcal{O}[[y_1, \ldots, y_g]] / (f_1, \ldots, f_{g-2})$$

where $f_1, \ldots, f_{g-2}$ are the relations coming from the obstruction space $H^2_\Sigma(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$.

**Smoothness criterion.** $R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}}$ is smooth (i.e., a power series ring) iff:
$$\dim H^2_\Sigma(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) = 0$$

For corank 2, this typically fails: $\dim H^2_\Sigma \geq 1$ by the global Euler characteristic. The deformation ring is therefore a **complete intersection** of the form:

$$R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}} \cong \mathcal{O}[[y_1, y_2, y_3]] / (f)$$

where $f$ is a single relation (when $\dim H^2_\Sigma = 1$). This is a hypersurface singularity.

**For GL$_3$ candidates.** For $n = 3$, the deformation ring has:
- $g \geq 8$ generators (from $\dim H^1_\Sigma(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) = 8$ for generic $\bar\rho$)
- $r = 2$ TW primes
- Relations from $H^2_\Sigma(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$, which can have dimension $\geq 3$

The predicted structure is:
$$R_\Sigma^{\mathrm{univ}} \cong \mathcal{O}[[y_1, \ldots, y_8]] / (f_1, f_2, f_3)$$

a complete intersection of codimension 3. The defect $\delta = \mathrm{pd}(M_\infty) - \dim(R_\infty)$ equals $\dim H^2_\Sigma(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) - \dim H^0(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1))$.

#### Congruence Ideal

The congruence ideal $\eta_\mathbb{T}$ satisfies:
$$\mathrm{length}_\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{O} / \eta_\mathbb{T}) = \mathrm{ord}_{s=1} L(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho, s)$$

For corank 2, $\mathrm{length}_\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{O} / \eta_\mathbb{T}) = 2$, so $\eta_\mathbb{T} = (\ell^2)$ (up to units). This means the adjoint L-function vanishes to order 2 at $s = 1$.

### 5. Summary and Open Problems

| Problem | Status | Difficulty |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Corank 2 for GL$_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ | Known (Calegari-Geraghty) | — |
| Corank 2 for GL$_2$ over totally real fields | Expected to follow from CG | Moderate |
| Corank 2 for GL$_n$ ($n \geq 3$) | Requires derived methods | High |
| Irregular weight cases | Open | Very high |
| Computational verification of deformation rings | Feasible for small $\ell, n$ | Moderate |
| $R = \mathbb{T}$ for complete intersections | Conditional on $\delta = 0$ | High |

The central open problem is: **Does the Calegari-Geraghty method suffice for corank $\geq 2$ when $n \geq 3$ and $H^2_\Sigma(\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) \neq 0$?** The answer requires controlling the derived Hecke algebra in the presence of a nontrivial obstruction space, which amounts to proving that the derived $R = \mathbb{T}$ isomorphism is compatible with the Fitting ideal filtration.
