# Group C: Arithmetic Statistics

## Mission

Study the average behavior of ranks, Tate-Shafarevich groups, and L-functions over families of elliptic curves. Rather than attacking individual curves, this group investigates what happens "on average" or "for 100% of curves" — leveraging the statistical structure of arithmetic objects to shed light on BSD where individual methods fall short. The central philosophy is that the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, while open in general, can be understood statistically: most curves behave well, and the pathological cases (high rank, large Sha) are rare.

## Scope

Each direction precisely identifies:
- The family of elliptic curves under consideration and how it is ordered
- The density notion used (natural density, ordering by conductor, height, or discriminant)
- Known bounds and thresholds
- The main obstruction to progress
- The specific connection to the BSD conjecture

## Cross-Group Connections

- **Group A (Iwasawa Theory):** Average behavior of $\mu$- and $\lambda$-invariants in families connects to Iwasawa-theoretic approaches to BSD.
- **Group B (Euler Systems):** Kolyvagin's method gives rank 0/1 BSD for individual curves; statistics show these cases are generic.
- **Group D (Tate-Shafarevich):** Cohen-Lenstra predictions for Sha complement the structural study of Sha.
- **Group H (Computational):** Large-scale verification of statistical predictions requires computational infrastructure.
