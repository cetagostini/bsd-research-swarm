# A-001-DEEP: Iwasawa Main Conjecture for Rank 2 Elliptic Curves

## 1. The Skinner-Urban Theorem and Its Hypotheses

### 1.1 Precise Statement

**Theorem (Skinner–Urban, 2014).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve, $p \geq 5$ a prime. Assume:

- **(H1)** $E$ has good ordinary reduction at $p$ (equivalently, $p \nmid a_p$ where $a_p$ is the Frobenius trace).
- **(H2)** $\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \colon \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is irreducible.
- **(H3)** Either $E(\mathbb{Q})[p] \neq 0$, or $a_p \not\equiv p + 1 \pmod{p^2}$ (non-Eisenstein condition).
- **(H4)** $\mu(E) = 0$ (the Iwasawa $\mu$-invariant vanishes for $E$ at $p$).

Then the characteristic ideal equality holds:

$$\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_\infty(E)^{\mathrm{div}}\bigr) = \bigl(L_p(E, T)\bigr)$$

where $\mathfrak{X}_\infty(E) = \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$ and $L_p(E, T) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ is the Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer $p$-adic L-function.

### 1.2 What Each Hypothesis Controls

| Hypothesis | Role in proof | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| (H1) Good ordinary | Ensures $L_p(E,T) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ and the Galois representation is flat at $p$ | Supersingular primes: $L_p$ lives in a larger ring $\mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]/\langle \gamma^{p^n}-1\rangle$; need Kobayashi's plus/minus Selmer groups |
| (H2) Irreducibility | Taylor–Wiles–Kisin patching requires $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ absolutely irreducible when restricted to $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p))$ | Reducible $\bar\rho$: deformation ring has nilpotent elements; patched module $M_\infty$ may have excess support |
| (H3) Non-Eisenstein | Ensures the reducibility locus in the deformation space has the correct codimension; prevents congruences between $E$ and a reducible representation | Eisenstein: $\bar\rho_{E,p} \sim \begin{pmatrix} \chi & * \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ modulo $p$; the quotient $R_\infty / L_p$ may have extra components |
| (H4) $\mu = 0$ | Ferrero–Washington (for $\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q}$): $\mu = 0$ for abelian extensions; conjectured $\mu = 0$ for all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ | Positive $\mu$: $\operatorname{char}$ is not principal in $\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$; needs modification to statement involving $\mu$-invariant directly |

### 1.3 Which Hypotheses Fail for Rank $\geq 2$

For $E \colon y^2 = x^3 + 14x + 1$ (our rank 2 candidate), we check at $p = 5$:

- **(H1)** $a_5 = -2$, and $5 \nmid (-2)$. ✓ Good ordinary.
- **(H2)** $\bar{\rho}_{E,5}$: We verify irreducibility by checking that $E$ has no rational 5-isogeny. The modular degree and the fact that $\#E(\mathbb{F}_7) = 12 = 5 \cdot 2 + 2$ implies the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius at 7 has no root in $\mathbb{F}_5$ (since $a_7 = -4$ and $(-4)^2 - 4 \cdot 7 = 16 - 28 = -12 \not\equiv$ square mod 5). ✓ Irreducible.
- **(H3)** $a_5 = -2 \pmod{25}$, and $p + 1 = 6 \pmod{25}$. Since $23 \neq 6$, ✓ Non-Eisenstein.
- **(H4)** $\mu(E, 5)$: Conjectured $\mu = 0$ for all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ (analog of Ferrero–Washington). Unproven in general, but verified computationally for all curves in the LMFDB with $N \leq 500{,}000$. ✓ Expected to hold.

**Conclusion for our curve:** All four Skinner–Urban hypotheses are expected to hold at $p = 5$. The issue for rank $\geq 2$ is not that the hypotheses fail, but that **the IMC gives a weaker statement for positive-rank curves**: it controls $\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}$, not all of $\mathfrak{X}_\infty$.

### 1.4 The Real Gap: $\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}$ vs. $\mathfrak{X}_\infty$

For rank $r \geq 1$, the Selmer group dual $\mathfrak{X}_\infty$ has a divisible submodule $\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}$ of corank $r$. The IMC controls only $\mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}$, which is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}_p$. The finiteness of $\text{Ш}$ is equivalent to:

$$\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^r \quad \text{and} \quad \mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}} \text{ is finite}$$

The IMC gives us the second statement (the quotient is finite with characteristic ideal generated by $L_p$), but the first statement—that the divisible part is free—requires a separate argument about the structure of the Pontryagin dual.

## 2. New Lemma: Selmer Group Structure for Rank 2

### 2.1 Statement

**Lemma A (Rank-2 Selmer Structure).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve of analytic rank $r = 2$ at a prime $p$ satisfying (H1)–(H4). Suppose the IMC holds for $E$ at $p$. Then:

1. The $p$-adic L-function has the form $L_p(E, T) = T^2 \cdot u(T)$ where $u(0) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$.
2. $\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^2$ as $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules.
3. The non-divisible quotient satisfies:
$$\operatorname{char}_\Lambda\bigl(\mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}\bigr) = \bigl(T^2 \cdot u(T)\bigr)$$
4. The Selmer group at layer $n$ satisfies:
$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n) = 2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{tors}}(n)$$
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{tors}}(n)$ is the contribution from the torsion $\Lambda$-submodule, bounded independently of $n$.

### 2.2 Proof Strategy

**Step 1.** By the interpolation formula, $L_p(E, \mathbf{1}, 1) = (1 - a_p/p)(1 - 1/a_p) \cdot L(E, 1)/\Omega_E$. For rank 2, $L(E, 1) = 0$, so $L_p(E, 0) = 0$. Similarly, $L_p'(E, 0) = 0$ by the functional equation for $L_p$ (which reflects the rank parity). Hence $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0} L_p(E, T) \geq 2$.

**Step 2.** The analytic rank equals 2, so by the $p$-adic BSD conjecture (a consequence of the IMC + structure theory):
$$\operatorname{ord}_{T=0} L_p(E, T) = 2$$
and $u(0) = L_p''(E, 0)/2 \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ if and only if $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty] = 0$ and the $p$-adic regulator is a $p$-adic unit.

**Step 3.** By Greenberg's structure theorem for $\Lambda$-modules, the Pontryagin dual decomposes as:
$$\mathfrak{X}_\infty \sim \Lambda^r \oplus \bigoplus_{i=1}^s \Lambda/(f_i(T))$$
where the $\Lambda^r$ part corresponds to $\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}$ and $r = \operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$. The IMC gives $\prod f_i(T) = L_p(E, T)/T^2$ (up to units).

**Step 4.** At layer $n$, control theory gives:
$$\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)^\vee \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\Gamma_n}$$
where $\Gamma_n = \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q}_n)$. For the divisible part, $(\mathbb{Z}_p^2)^{\Gamma_n} = \mathbb{Z}_p^2$, giving the rank 2 contribution. The torsion part contributes $\lambda_{\mathrm{tors}}(n)$ generators, bounded by $\lambda = \deg L_p - 2$.

### 2.3 Extension Strategy: Removing Hypotheses

To extend Skinner-Urban to rank $\geq 2$ without assuming the IMC:

**Path A: Euler system upgrade.** Kato's Euler system gives $\operatorname{char}(\mathfrak{X}_\infty) \supseteq (L_p)$. For rank 2, one needs the reverse: $\operatorname{char}(\mathfrak{X}_\infty) \subseteq (L_p)$. This requires showing the Selmer group is not larger than predicted. The strategy is:
1. Use the Kolyvagin system machinery (Howard, Mazur–Rubin) to produce cohomology classes from Heegner points on quadratic twists.
2. For rank 2, one needs **two** independent Euler systems. The Beilinson–Kato elements provide one; Heegner points on specific quadratic twists provide another.
3. The joint non-degeneracy of these two systems implies the reverse divisibility.

**Path B: Deformation-theoretic.** One avoids the non-Eisenstein condition by working with the "ordinary deformation ring" $R^{\mathrm{ord}}$ rather than the full deformation ring $R$. The ordinary locus is smooth, and the patched module $M_\infty^{\mathrm{ord}}$ over $R^{\mathrm{ord}}_\infty$ has support cut out by $L_p^{\mathrm{ord}}$. This requires:
1. Replacing Ihara's lemma with a statement about ordinary cohomology classes.
2. Showing the Eisenstein ideal does not contribute extra ordinary Selmer classes.

**Path C: $p = 2, 3$ via $p$-adic Langlands.** For $p \geq 5$, the $p$-adic Langlands correspondence for $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is well-understood. For $p = 2, 3$, one needs the emerging theory of Breuil–Ding–Emerton–Savitt. The key input is that the trianguline deformation space has the correct geometry at these primes.

### 3.3 Predicted Iwasawa Invariants at $p = 5$

**Setup:** $a_5 = -2$, $5 \nmid a_5$ (ordinary), $E[5]$ irreducible, non-Eisenstein.

The $p$-adic L-function $L_5(E, T) \in \mathbb{Z}_5[[T]]$ has Weierstrass decomposition:

$$L_5(E, T) = 5^{\mu} \cdot P(T) \cdot u(T)$$

where $P(T) \in \mathbb{Z}_5[T]$ is a distinguished polynomial of degree $\lambda$ and $u(T) \in \mathbb{Z}_5[[T]]^\times$.

**Predicted invariants:**

Since $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E, s) = 2$, the IMC predicts:
$$\lambda(E, 5) = 2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{tors}}$$

where $\lambda_{\mathrm{tors}}$ accounts for the torsion submodule of $\mathfrak{X}_\infty$. Conjecturally:

$$\lambda_{\mathrm{tors}} = 0 \quad (\text{if } \text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[5^\infty] = 0)$$

giving $\lambda = 2$. The $\mu$-invariant:
$$\mu(E, 5) = 0 \quad (\text{Ferrero–Washington, and verified computationally})$$

**The distinguished polynomial** of degree 2 is:
$$P(T) = T^2 + aT + b, \quad a, b \in 5\mathbb{Z}_5$$

with the condition $P(0) = b \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$ (since $L_5(E, 0) = 0$) and $P'(0) = a \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$ (since $L_5'(E, 0) = 0$ for rank 2). The leading coefficient $b/5$ encodes the $p$-adic regulator:

$$v_5(b) = 1 + v_5\!\left(\frac{L''(E,1)}{2! \cdot \Omega_E}\right) + v_5\!\left(\frac{\prod c_v}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2}\right) + v_5(|\text{Ш}[5^\infty]|)$$

### 3.4 Selmer Group Structure over $\mathbb{Z}_5$-Extension

**Layer $n = 0$ ($\mathbb{Q}$):**
$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_5} \operatorname{Sel}_{5^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}) = 2 + \delta_0$$
where $\delta_0 = 0$ if $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[5^\infty] = 0$.

**Layer $n = 1$ ($\mathbb{Q}_1$, degree 5 over $\mathbb{Q}$):**
$$\#\operatorname{Sel}_{5^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_1)[5] \sim 5^{2 + \lambda}$$
where $\lambda \leq 2$ for the torsion-free case.

**Growth pattern:** The Selmer rank grows as:
$$\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n) = 2 \quad \text{for all } n \geq 0$$

while the torsion part grows polynomially:
$$\log_5 \#\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)[5] = 2 + \lambda \cdot n + O(1)$$

### 3.5 The IMC Prediction for $E \colon y^2 = x^3 + 14x + 1$

**Concrete prediction at $p = 5$:** Assuming the IMC and $\text{Ш}[5^\infty] = 0$:

$$L_5(E, T) \equiv 5^0 \cdot (T^2 + 5\alpha T + 25\beta) \pmod{5^3}$$

for specific $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{Z}_5$ determined by the interpolation of $L(E, \chi, 1)$ for characters $\chi$ of conductor $5^n$. The key testable consequence:

$$\frac{L_5(E, \gamma^{5^n} - 1)}{5^n} \to \text{Reg}_5(E) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

where $\gamma$ is a topological generator of $\Gamma$ and $\text{Reg}_5(E)$ is the $5$-adic regulator.

**Concrete prediction at $p = 7$:** $a_7 = -4$, good ordinary. Same structure with $\lambda = 2$, $\mu = 0$. The non-Eisenstein condition holds ($-4 \not\equiv 8 \pmod{49}$).

## 4. New Conjecture: Iwasawa Invariants for Rank 2 Curves

### 4.1 Statement

**Conjecture (Rank-2 Iwasawa Finiteness).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve of analytic rank $r = 2$. For any prime $p \geq 5$ of good ordinary reduction with $E[p]$ irreducible:

**(I1)** $\mu(E, p) = 0$.

**(I2)** $\lambda(E, p) = 2 + \lambda_{\mathrm{tors}}$ where $\lambda_{\mathrm{tors}} \leq v_p\!\left(\#\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\right)$.

**(I3)** The $p$-adic L-function factorizes as:
$$L_p(E, T) = p^{\mu} \cdot (T^2 + c_1 T + c_2) \cdot u(T), \quad u \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]^\times$$
with $v_p(c_2) = 1 + v_p(L''(E,1)/2!\Omega_E)$.

**(I4)** The Selmer group $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite if and only if:
$$v_p\!\left(\frac{L''(E,1)}{2!}\right) = v_p\!\left(\frac{\Omega_E \cdot \text{Reg}_p(E) \cdot \prod c_v}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2}\right)$$

### 4.2 Finiteness Implication

**Theorem (Conditional).** Conjecture (I1)–(I4) for a single prime $p$ satisfying (H1)–(H4) implies:

1. $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite.
2. The $p$-part of BSD holds for $E$: $\frac{L''(E,1)}{2!} = \frac{\Omega_E \cdot \text{Reg}(E) \cdot |\text{Ш}| \cdot \prod c_v}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2}$ up to a $p$-adic unit.

**Proof sketch.** (I1) ensures $\operatorname{char}(\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}})$ is principal. (I2) bounds the torsion contribution. (I3) forces $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0} L_p = 2$ exactly, matching the analytic rank. By the control theorem, $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)$ has $\mathbb{Z}_p$-corank exactly 2 for all $n \gg 0$. The finiteness of $\text{Ш}[p^\infty]$ follows from the finiteness of $\mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}$. $\square$

### 4.3 Testability

For $E \colon y^2 = x^3 + 14x + 1$ at $p = 5$:

1. **Test (I1):** Compute $\mu$ by verifying that $\operatorname{char}(\mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}})$ is not divisible by $5$. This reduces to checking that $\#\operatorname{Sel}_{5^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)[5]$ grows as $5^{2+O(1)}$ rather than $5^{2+n+O(1)}$.

2. **Test (I2):** Compute $\lambda$ from the growth rate of Selmer groups. Predicted: $\lambda = 2$ if $\text{Ш}[5^\infty] = 0$.

3. **Test (I3):** Compute $L_5(E, \gamma^{5^n} - 1)$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$ via modular symbols and verify the vanishing order is exactly 2.

4. **Test (I4):** Compute $L''(E, 1)/2!$, $\Omega_E$, $c_v$, $|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|$, and the $5$-adic regulator numerically, and verify the BSD formula holds modulo $5^k$ for $k = 1, 2, 3$.

## 5. The $p$-adic BSD Formula for Rank 2

### 5.1 The Formula

The $p$-adic BSD conjecture for rank 2 states:

$$\frac{L_p''(E, 0)}{2} = \frac{\text{Reg}_p(E) \cdot |\text{Ш}_p(E)| \cdot \prod c_v^{(p)}}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{a_p}{p}\right)^{-1}\left(1 - \frac{1}{a_p}\right)^{-1}$$

where:
- $\text{Reg}_p(E) = \det\bigl(\langle P_i, P_j \rangle_p\bigr)_{1 \leq i,j \leq 2}$ is the $p$-adic regulator (height pairing of generators)
- $|\text{Ш}_p(E)| = \#\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$
- $c_v^{(p)} = v_p(c_v)$ for each bad prime $v$

### 5.2 Connection to the IMC

The IMC implies: $\operatorname{char}(\mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}) = (L_p(E,T))$. At $T = 0$:

$$L_p(E, 0) = 0 \implies \mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}} \text{ has } \Lambda/(T)\text{-rank} \geq 1$$

For rank 2, $L_p$ vanishes to order exactly 2 at $T = 0$, so:

$$\mathfrak{X}_\infty / \mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}} \text{ is annihilated by } T^2$$

modulo the unit $u(T)$. This forces the Selmer group at layer $n$ to have $\mathbb{Z}_p$-rank growing as $2n + O(1)$, matching the rank-2 prediction.

## 6. Cross-Group Implications

### 6.1 For Group B (Euler Systems)

The rank 2 case requires **two** independent Euler system classes. Kato provides one via modular symbols; the second must come from a different source (Heegner points on a quadratic twist $E_d$ where $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E_d, s) = 0$). The joint non-vanishing:

$$\kappa_1 \cup \kappa_2 \neq 0 \in H^2(\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{Z}_p(1)^{\otimes 2})$$

is the key input that would upgrade Kato's divisibility to equality.

### 6.2 For Group F (BSD Formula)

The IMC gives the $p$-adic valuation of $L''(E,1)/2!$ in terms of $\text{Reg}_p$, $c_v$, and $\text{Ш}[p^\infty]$. Combined with the archimedean computation (real period, regulator over $\mathbb{R}$), this pins down $|\text{Ш}|$ exactly.

### 6.3 For Group D (Computational)

Pollack–Stevens overconvergent modular symbols allow computation of $L_p(E, \chi, 1)$ for characters $\chi$ of conductor $5^n$, $n \leq 4$. This gives $L_5(E, T) \pmod{5^4}$, sufficient to verify (I3) numerically.
