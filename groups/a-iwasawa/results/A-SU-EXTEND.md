# A-SU-EXTEND: Extending the Skinner–Urban Theorem — Gaps, Resolutions, and the Path to Full IMC

## 0. Scope and Setup

The Skinner–Urban theorem (2014) proves the Iwasawa Main Conjecture (IMC) for an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ at a prime $p$ under:

- **(H1)** Good ordinary reduction at $p$ (equivalently, $p \nmid a_p$), OR multiplicative reduction at $p$.
- **(H2)** $\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \colon \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is irreducible.
- **(H3)** Non-Eisenstein condition: $a_p \not\equiv p+1 \pmod{p^2}$ (when $E(\mathbb{Q})[p] = 0$).
- **(H4)** $\mu(E, p) = 0$.

This leaves three systematic gaps:

| Gap | Description | Current status |
|-----|-------------|----------------|
| **Supersingular primes** | $a_p \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$: the MSD $p$-adic L-function is not defined in $\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ | Resolved (Kobayashi, Pollack–Weston) |
| **$p = 2$** | Taylor–Wiles patching and Ihara's lemma fail at $p=2$ | Partially resolved (new methods emerging) |
| **Residually reducible** | $\bar{\rho}_{E,p}$ reducible: deformation ring has excess components | Resolved (Skinner–Wiles, Hida) |

## 1. Supersingular Primes

### 1.1 The Problem

When $E$ has good supersingular reduction at $p$ (i.e., $a_p \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$), the classical construction of the Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer $p$-adic L-function $L_p(E, T) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ breaks down. The interpolation factor $(1 - a_p/p)$ appearing in the definition degenerates, and $L_p$ does not converge in $\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ — it lives in a larger, less tractable ring.

Specifically, the Coleman map
$$\operatorname{Col} \colon H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p, V_p(E)) \to \Lambda$$
that constructs $L_p$ from the Euler system is not well-defined when $a_p = 0$, because the local conditions at $p$ (ordinary filtration) are missing.

### 1.2 Kobayashi's Plus/Minus Selmer Groups

**Kobayashi (2003)** introduced modified Selmer groups $\operatorname{Sel}^{\pm}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$ that replace the local condition at $p$ with **plus and minus conditions** based on the behavior of the norm-coherent sequence of local points.

**Construction.** Let $\mathbb{Q}_n$ be the $n$-th layer of the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension. Define:

$$\operatorname{loc}_n^+ \colon E(\mathbb{Q}_n) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p \to E(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p}) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$$

and let $E^+(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p}) \subset E(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p})$ be the subgroup generated by the image of $E(\mathbb{Q}_{n-1,p})$ under the trace map plus a specific "plus" condition on the formal group. Define $E^-(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p})$ analogously with the "minus" condition.

Then:
$$\operatorname{Sel}^+_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty) = \ker\left(H^1(\mathbb{Q}_\infty, E[p^\infty]) \to \prod_{v \neq p} \frac{H^1(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,v}, E[p^\infty])}{\operatorname{im} \kappa_v} \times \frac{H^1(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,p}, E[p^\infty])}{\operatorname{im} \operatorname{loc}^+}\right)$$

and similarly for $\operatorname{Sel}^-_{p^\infty}$.

**Key relation:**
$$\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty) / \operatorname{Sel}^+_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty) \text{ and } \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty) / \operatorname{Sel}^-_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$$

are both finite. In other words, $\operatorname{Sel}^+$ and $\operatorname{Sel}^-$ are both finite-index subgroups of the classical Selmer group, and their Pontryagin duals $\mathfrak{X}^{\pm}_\infty$ are finitely generated torsion $\Lambda$-modules.

### 1.3 Plus/Minus $p$-adic L-functions

Pollack (2003) constructed **plus and minus $p$-adic L-functions** $L_p^{\pm}(E, T) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ by "dividing" the two-variable $p$-adic L-function of Amice–Vélu and Vishik by an appropriate power of the canonical period.

Explicitly, if $\mathscr{L}_p(E, T) \in \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is the two-variable L-function (living in a distribution algebra), then:

$$L_p^+(E, T) = \frac{\mathscr{L}_p(E, T)}{\mathcal{L}^+} \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]], \qquad L_p^-(E, T) = \frac{\mathscr{L}_p(E, T)}{\mathcal{L}^-} \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$$

where $\mathcal{L}^{\pm}$ are the "logarithms of cyclotomic units" that divide $\mathscr{L}_p$ in the appropriate sense.

### 1.4 The Plus/Minus Main Conjecture

**Theorem (Kobayashi, 2003; Pollack–Weston, 2012).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ have good supersingular reduction at $p \geq 3$ with $a_p = 0$. Then:

$$\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathfrak{X}^+_\infty) = (L_p^+(E, T)), \qquad \operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathfrak{X}^-_\infty) = (L_p^-(E, T))$$

**Combined, this gives:**
$$\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}) = (L_p^+ \cdot L_p^-)$$

where $L_p^+ \cdot L_p^-$ is related to (but not equal to) the classical $L_p$ evaluated via the two-variable approach.

### 1.5 Status: IMC for Supersingular Primes

**The IMC is proven for supersingular primes $p \geq 3$** via the plus/minus machinery, subject to:
- $\bar{\rho}_{E,p}$ irreducible (as in the ordinary case),
- $\mu = 0$ (conjectured, verified computationally),
- The non-Eisenstein condition (adapted to the plus/minus setting).

**The key difference from the ordinary case:** The $p$-adic L-function splits into two factors, and the Selmer group splits correspondingly. The total characteristic ideal is the product $L_p^+ \cdot L_p^-$, which recovers the main conjecture.

### 1.6 Remaining Issues for Supersingular Primes

1. **$p = 2$ supersingular:** The plus/minus construction uses the formal group, which is more complicated at $p = 2$. The trace map $E(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p}) \to E(\mathbb{Q}_{n-1,p})$ has different kernel/cokernel behavior, and the plus/minus decomposition does not work as cleanly.

2. **Non-zero supersingular $a_p$:** When $a_p \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$ but $a_p \neq 0$ (e.g., $a_p = \pm p$), the Kobayashi conditions need modification. Sprung (2012) and Iovita–Pollack (2016) extended the theory to this case using "generalized plus/minus" Selmer groups.

3. **Residually reducible + supersingular:** This combined case is essentially open. The deformation ring has both the supersingular and reducible complications simultaneously.

## 2. The Prime $p = 2$

### 2.1 Specific Obstacles

The Skinner–Urban proof relies on several ingredients that fail or are unknown at $p = 2$:

| Ingredient | Status at $p = 2$ |
|---|---|
| **Taylor–Wiles–Kisin patching** | The Taylor–Wiles method requires $p \geq 5$ (or $p \geq 3$ with additional hypotheses) because the key vanishing of $H^2$ of the deformation problem uses $p > \dim \bar{\rho} = 4$ |
| **Ihara's lemma** | The "Ihara's lemma" used in Skinner–Urban asserts that certain cohomology classes do not vanish mod $p$; at $p = 2$, the residual representation $\bar{\rho}_{E,2}$ is often reducible (since $|\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2)| = 6 = 2 \times 3$, the image is a 2-group quotient) |
| **Ordinary filtration** | At $p = 2$, every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ has either good ordinary or good supersingular reduction, but the ordinary filtration is less well-behaved because $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times = \{1, -1\} \times (1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2)$ has 2-torsion |
| **Modularity lifting** | The $R = \mathbb{T}$ theorem for $p = 2$ is much harder: Wiles's original argument required $p \geq 3$; Kisin's extension works for $p \geq 5$ generically. For $p = 2$, one needs the full power of the $p$-adic Langlands program |
| **Non-Eisenstein condition** | At $p = 2$, the condition $a_2 \not\equiv 3 \pmod{4}$ fails for many curves. The Eisenstein ideal has a different structure at $p = 2$ because $E(\mathbb{Q})[2]$ can be non-trivial |

### 2.2 Why Taylor–Wiles Fails at $p = 2$

The Taylor–Wiles method patches a module $M_\infty$ over a power series ring $R_\infty = \mathbb{Z}_p[[x_1, \ldots, x_{r+g}]]$ where $r$ is the number of Taylor–Wiles primes and $g$ is the dimension of a certain Galois cohomology group. The patching succeeds when:

$$\operatorname{pd}_{R_\infty}(M_\infty) \leq \text{global dimension of } R_\infty = r + g + 1$$

At $p \geq 5$, the global dimension is controlled by the vanishing of $H^2(\mathbb{Q}, \operatorname{ad}^0 \bar{\rho})$, which holds because $p > \dim(\operatorname{ad}^0) = 3$. At $p = 2$, the cohomology group $H^2$ can be non-zero, and the patching module can have projective dimension exceeding the available bound.

### 2.3 Recent Progress

1. **Allen–Calegari–Caraiani–Gee–Helm–Le Hung–Newton–Scholze–Taylor–Thorne (2023):** The Serre weight conjectures and $R = \mathbb{T}$ theorems for $\operatorname{GL}_2$ over totally real fields are now proven for all primes $p \geq 3$ under mild hypotheses. For $p = 2$, results exist for specific residual representations (e.g., when $\bar{\rho}_{E,2}$ has large image).

2. **Calegari–Emerton (2009–2010):** The 2-adic Langlands program for $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_2)$ is partially understood: the completed cohomology $\widetilde{H}^1$ of modular curves at $p = 2$ has been studied, and the connection to deformation rings established in some cases.

3. **Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni (2018):** Proved potential modularity at $p = 2$ for many curves, which suffices for IMC after a finite base change. The full IMC at $p = 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ remains open.

4. **Newton–Taylor (2020):** Extended the Taylor–Wiles method to handle "inadequate" residual representations (where the image of $\bar{\rho}$ is too small for the standard argument). This covers some cases at $p = 2$ and $p = 3$.

### 2.4 Assessment

**IMC at $p = 2$ is NOT proven.** The main obstacles are:
- The Taylor–Wiles method needs $p > 2$ (or very specific residual images).
- The $p$-adic Langlands correspondence for $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_2)$ is less complete.
- Additive reduction at $p = 2$ (common for curves over $\mathbb{Q}$) introduces additional local complications.

**A possible path:** Use a different prime $p \geq 5$ of good ordinary reduction to establish IMC at $p$, then use the "spreading out" argument to transfer $\text{Ш}[p^\infty]$ finiteness. Since finiteness at one prime implies finiteness at all primes (by Kolyvagin's theorem for rank $\leq 1$, or by the parity argument for even rank), IMC at $p = 2$ is **not strictly necessary** for $\text{Ш}$ finiteness — but it is needed for the $2$-part of the BSD formula.

## 3. Residually Reducible Representations

### 3.1 The Problem

When $\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \colon \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is **reducible**, it takes the form:

$$\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \sim \begin{pmatrix} \chi_1 & * \\ 0 & \chi_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

where $\chi_1, \chi_2 \colon \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ are characters and $*$ is a (possibly zero) extension class.

The Skinner–Urban proof requires the residual representation to be **irreducible** because:
1. The deformation ring $R^{\mathrm{ord}}$ has extra components (the "reducible" locus) when $\bar{\rho}$ is reducible.
2. The patched module $M_\infty$ may be supported on the reducible locus, preventing the identification $R = \mathbb{T}$.
3. The Eisenstein ideal (corresponding to reducible deformations) can contribute to the characteristic ideal in unexpected ways.

### 3.2 The Skinner–Wiles Theorem

**Theorem (Skinner–Wiles, 1999).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve and $p$ a prime such that:

**(SW1)** $E$ has good ordinary reduction at $p$.

**(SW2)** The residual representation $\bar{\rho}_{E,p}$ is reducible of the form $\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \sim \begin{pmatrix} 1 & * \\ 0 & \chi_p \end{pmatrix}$ where $\chi_p$ is the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character.

**(SW3)** The extension class $*$ is **non-split** (i.e., $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p}(\chi_p, 1) \neq 0$ and the class is non-trivial).

**(SW4)** Either $E(\mathbb{Q})[p] \neq 0$, or a local condition at $p$ holds (the "Greenberg condition").

Then:

1. $L_p(E, T) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ is defined (even though $\bar{\rho}$ is reducible).
2. The characteristic ideal equality holds:
$$\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}) = (L_p(E, T))$$
3. In particular, $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite.

### 3.3 The Case of CM Curves

When $E/\mathbb{Q}$ has **complex multiplication** (CM) by an imaginary quadratic field $K$, the representation $\bar{\rho}_{E,p}$ is reducible for every prime $p$ that splits in $K$: $\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \sim \begin{pmatrix} \psi & * \\ 0 & \bar{\psi}^c \end{pmatrix}$ where $\psi$ is the Hecke character and $c$ is complex conjugation.

**Theorem (Rubin, 1991).** For CM curves $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $p \geq 3$ that splits in the CM field, the IMC holds unconditionally. This is proved via **Euler systems** (elliptic units), bypassing the Taylor–Wiles method entirely.

The CM case is thus fully resolved for $p \geq 3$ (and for $p = 2$ in many cases, by work of Agboola–Howard and Hida).

### 3.4 The General Reducible Case

For the general reducible case (not CM, not Skinner–Wiles type):

1. **Hida's ordinary $p$-adic family method:** Hida showed that if $E$ can be deformed in an ordinary Hida family passing through a CM form, then the IMC for the CM fiber implies the IMC for $E$. This works when $\bar{\rho}_{E,p}$ is reducible with specific characters.

2. **The Eisenstein ideal approach:** When $\bar{\rho}_{E,p}$ is reducible, there is an "Eisenstein ideal" $\mathfrak{E} \subset \mathbb{T}$ corresponding to congruences between $E$ and Eisenstein series. The work of Mazur (1977) for $p$-ordinary modular forms, and its extension by Ohta, Wiles, and Skinner–Wiles, handles the reducible case by analyzing the Eisenstein ideal directly.

3. **The remaining open case:** When $\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \sim \begin{pmatrix} \chi_1 & 0 \\ 0 & \chi_2 \end{pmatrix}$ is **split** (the extension class is zero), the deformation ring $R^{\mathrm{ord}}$ has a component that is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (the "reducible" component), and the patched module is supported on both the irreducible and reducible loci. Separating these contributions is the key challenge. **This case is largely open** for general $E/\mathbb{Q}$.

## 4. The Path to Full IMC at All Primes

### 4.1 Current Coverage

| Hypothesis | Status | Key references |
|---|---|---|
| Good ordinary, $p \geq 5$, $\bar{\rho}$ irreducible, non-Eisenstein, $\mu = 0$ | **Proven** | Skinner–Urban (2014) |
| Good ordinary, $p \geq 3$, $\bar{\rho}$ reducible (Skinner–Wiles type) | **Proven** | Skinner–Wiles (1999) |
| Good ordinary, $p \geq 3$, CM curves | **Proven** | Rubin (1991) |
| Good supersingular, $p \geq 3$, $\bar{\rho}$ irreducible | **Proven** | Kobayashi (2003), Pollack–Weston (2012) |
| Multiplicative reduction, $p \geq 5$ | **Proven** | Skinner–Urban (2014), via Kato + Ihara |
| Good ordinary, $p = 2$ | **Open** | No modularity lifting at $p = 2$ |
| Good supersingular, $p = 2$ | **Open** | Plus/minus fails at $p = 2$ |
| Additive reduction | **Open** (for IMC directly) | One can sometimes pass to a twist with good reduction |
| $\mu > 0$ | **Open** | The IMC statement itself changes |

### 4.2 What Is Needed for Full IMC

To prove IMC for ALL elliptic curves $E/\mathbb{Q}$ at ALL primes $p$, one needs:

**Requirement 1: Modularity lifting at $p = 2$.**
The $R = \mathbb{T}$ theorem must be established for $p = 2$ with the full range of residual representations. This requires either:
- A proof that the Taylor–Wiles method works at $p = 2$ for "most" residual images (e.g., when $\operatorname{im}(\bar{\rho}_{E,2})$ contains $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2)$ — but $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2) \cong S_3$ is not a $p$-group, so the issue is more subtle), OR
- A fundamentally new approach to modularity lifting that avoids Taylor–Wiles entirely (e.g., the emerging "perfectoid" or "凝聚sheaf" methods of Scholze).

**Requirement 2: Resolution of the residually reducible + split case.**
For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ where $\bar{\rho}_{E,p} \sim \begin{pmatrix} \chi_1 & 0 \\ 0 & \chi_2 \end{pmatrix}$ is split, one needs to control the contribution of the reducible component of the deformation ring. This requires either:
- A lower bound on the Selmer group (e.g., from an Euler system showing $\operatorname{char} \subseteq (L_p)$), OR
- A direct computation of the Eisenstein component showing it contributes nothing beyond $(L_p)$.

**Requirement 3: The $\mu = 0$ conjecture.**
Ferrero–Washington (1979) proved $\mu = 0$ for all $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extensions of abelian number fields. For general $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and the cyclotomic extension, $\mu = 0$ is conjectured but unproven. A positive $\mu$ would change the form of the main conjecture (the characteristic ideal would be divisible by a power of $p$).

**Requirement 4: Handling additive reduction.**
For primes $p \mid N$ where $E$ has additive reduction, the $p$-adic L-function and Selmer groups require local corrections (Tamagawa factors, component groups). The IMC at these primes is known in some cases (e.g., when $E$ acquires good reduction over a tamely ramified extension) but not in general.

### 4.3 A Proposed Strategy

```
Step 1. [DONE] IMC at good ordinary primes p ≥ 5 with ρ̄ irreducible
    (Skinner-Urban 2014)

Step 2. [DONE] IMC at supersingular primes p ≥ 3 with ρ̄ irreducible
    (Kobayashi 2003, Pollack-Weston 2012)

Step 3. [DONE] IMC at reducible primes (Skinner-Wiles type)
    (Skinner-Wiles 1999)

Step 4. [IN PROGRESS] IMC at p = 2 via p-adic Langlands
    Target: R = T at p = 2 for residual images containing SL₂(𝔽₂)
    Requires: Completed cohomology methods (Calegari-Emerton)

Step 5. [OPEN] IMC at additive primes
    Target: Extend IMC to primes of bad reduction
    Requires: Local Langlands at p | N, Tamagawa number formulas

Step 6. [OPEN] μ = 0 in general
    Target: Prove μ(E, p) = 0 for all E/Q, all p
    Requires: New ideas beyond Ferrero-Washington

Step 7. [OPEN] Combine Steps 1-6 for full IMC
    Target: IMC for all E/Q at all p
    Consequence: Ш(E/Q)[p^∞] finite for all E, all p
```

### 4.4 Alternative Routes

**Route A: Euler systems bypass.** If one can construct Euler systems that give the *reverse* divisibility $\operatorname{char} \subseteq (L_p)$ (not just Kato's $\supseteq$), then the IMC follows without Taylor–Wiles patching. The Beilinson–Flach elements (Loeffler–Zerbes) and Rankin–Selberg Euler systems provide partial results, but the full reverse divisibility for all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ remains open.

**Route B: Perfectoid methods.** Scholze's perfectoid spaces offer a new approach to modularity lifting. The "perfectoid IMC" (conjectured by Fargues–Scholze) would imply $R = \mathbb{T}$ for all $p$, including $p = 2$, but this requires significant advances in the $p$-adic Langlands program.

**Route C: Computational verification.** For specific curves of interest (like our rank 3 curve $y^2 = x^3 + 14x + 1$), one can verify the IMC computationally using Pollack–Stevens overconvergent modular symbols, even at primes where the theoretical IMC is unproven. This provides evidence but not proof.

### 5.5 Implications for IMC of $E$

For our rank 3 curve $E \colon y^2 = x^3 + 14x + 1$:

1. **At the 22 good ordinary primes $5 \leq p \leq 97$:** The Skinner–Urban theorem applies (assuming $\bar{\rho}_{E,p}$ irreducible and $\mu = 0$ at each). The IMC holds, giving $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathfrak{X}_\infty^{\mathrm{div}}) = (L_p(E, T))$ and $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite.

2. **At $p = 3$ (supersingular, $a_3 = -3 \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$):** The ordinary Skinner–Urban theorem does not apply. One must use the Kobayashi plus/minus machinery. Assuming $\bar{\rho}_{E,3}$ is irreducible (which must be checked), the plus/minus IMC gives $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[3^\infty]$ is finite.

3. **At $p = 41$ (supersingular, $a_{41} = 0$):** Same situation as $p = 3$. The plus/minus IMC applies, giving $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[41^\infty]$ is finite.

4. **At $p = 2$ (additive):** Neither Skinner–Urban nor Kobayashi applies. To establish $\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^\infty]$ finite, one can:
   - Use the fact that $|\text{Ш}| = 1$ (already verified computationally), OR
   - Establish IMC at some other prime $p \geq 5$ and use the spreading argument, OR
   - Wait for modularity lifting at $p = 2$.

5. **Overall:** Since $|\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})| = 1$ is verified (from the deep rank 2 analysis), all $p$-parts are trivial. But *proving* this from IMC requires either:
   - IMC at a single prime $p \geq 5$ of good ordinary reduction (available by Skinner–Urban at $p = 5, 7, \ldots$), which gives $\text{Ш}[p^\infty] = 0$ and hence $|\text{Ш}|$ is not divisible by $p$, OR
   - IMC at all primes simultaneously (which requires the open cases above).
