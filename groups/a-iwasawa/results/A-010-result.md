# A-010: Refined Iwasawa Theory with Euler System Input

## Status Assessment

The refined Main Conjecture (Fitting ideal version) is the strongest form of the Iwasawa Main Conjecture and directly implies the $p$-adic BSD formula. It is proven in the CM case by Rubin (1991) and in certain "binary" settings by Howard (2006). For non-CM curves, it is open but Kato's Euler system provides the key input. Realistic timeline: 8–12 years for the non-CM case.

## Testable Conjecture

**Conjecture (Fitting Ideal Main Conjecture).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at $p$ and $E[p]$ irreducible. Let $\kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, T_p E) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ be Kato's Euler system class. Then

$$\operatorname{Fitt}_\Lambda\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee\right) = \left(L_p(E, T)\right)$$

and furthermore, $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$ is generated by the image of $\kappa$ under the localization map $H^1(\mathbb{Q}, T_p E) \to H^1(\mathbb{Q}_\infty, T_p E)$. The Pontryagin pairing identifies $\mathfrak{X}_\infty$ with an ideal $I_\kappa \subseteq \Lambda$, and

$$\exp_p^*(\kappa) = L_p(E, T) \cdot u_\kappa \quad \text{for some } u_\kappa \in \Lambda^\times$$

where $\exp_p^*$ is the dual exponential map at $p$.

## Approach Summary

**Phase 1: Kato's Euler system at finite layers.** Compute the Kato classes $z_n = \kappa|_{\mathbb{Q}_n} \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}_n, T_p E)$ for layers $n = 0, 1, \ldots, 5$ of the cyclotomic tower. Verify norm-compatibility: $\operatorname{cor}_{n+1}^n(z_{n+1}) = a_p \cdot z_n$ (the Frobenius trace controls the norm).

**Phase 2: Fitting ideal computation.** At each layer $n$, compute $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)^\vee)$ as a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module. The Fitting ideal is generated by $p^k$ where $k$ is the minimal number of generators of the dual Selmer group. Compare with $L_p(E, \zeta_{p^n} - 1)$ evaluated at the layer-$n$ character.

**Phase 3: Rubin–Kolyvagin structure theorem.** Apply the general Euler system machinery (Rubin's method as exposited by Mazur–Rubin) to Kato's classes. The key inputs are: (a) the local condition at $p$ (the class $z_n$ maps to the Kummer image of a global point under $\exp_p^*$), and (b) the non-degeneracy of the global pairing (the Cassels–Tate pairing on the Selmer group). If $z_n$ is non-torsion, it generates a rank-1 submodule of $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)$, and the Kolyvagin derivative classes bound the Selmer group from above.

## Computational Example

**Curve 11a1** at $p = 5$ ($a_5 = -2$, rank 0):

Kato's class $\kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, T_p E)$. At layer $n = 0$: $z_0 = \kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, T_5 E)$. The image under $\exp_5^*: H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_5, V_5 E) \to D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V_5 E)/\mathrm{Fil}^0 \cong \mathbb{Q}_5$ is:

$$\exp_5^*(z_0) = L_5(11a1, 1) = (1 - a_5/5)(1 - 1/a_5) \cdot L(11a1, 1)/\Omega = (1 + 2/5)(1 + 1/2) \cdot 0.2538/\Omega_{11a1}$$

This is a 5-adic unit times $L(11a1, 1)/\Omega$. Since $L(11a1, 1) \neq 0$, the class $z_0$ is non-torsion, and $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_5}(\operatorname{Sel}_{5^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})^\vee) = (L_5(11a1, 1))$, a 5-adic unit ideal.

**Curve 37a1** at $p = 3$ ($a_3 = -2$, rank 1):

$L_p(37a1, 0) = 0$ since $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(37a1, s) = 1$. The Kato class $z_0$ maps to 0 under $\exp_3^*$ (since the $L$-value vanishes). But the derivative $L_p'(37a1, 0) \neq 0$, and the "derived" Kato class (obtained by differentiating in the cyclotomic direction) should generate $\operatorname{Sel}_{3^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$.

## Obstacle Analysis

The gap between Fitting and characteristic ideals is the main obstacle. For a $\Lambda$-module $M$, $\operatorname{Fitt}_\Lambda(M) \subseteq \operatorname{char}_\Lambda(M)$ always, with equality if and only if $M$ is $\Lambda$-cyclic (generated by a single element). For Selmer groups, cyclicity is not guaranteed: the Selmer group may require multiple generators, in which case the Fitting ideal is strictly smaller. The characteristic ideal equality (Kato + Skinner–Urban) gives $\operatorname{char}(\mathfrak{X}) = (L_p)$, but upgrading to $\operatorname{Fitt}(\mathfrak{X}) = (L_p)$ requires showing that $\mathfrak{X}$ is cyclic. A potential strategy: show that the Pontryagin pairing $\langle \kappa, - \rangle: \mathfrak{X} \to \Lambda$ is surjective onto the ideal $(L_p)$, which would imply cyclicity. This is related to the "explicit reciprocity law" of Perrin-Riou.

## Cross-Group Connections

- **Group A (Main Conjecture):** The Fitting ideal version strengthens the characteristic ideal equality.
- **Group C (Euler Systems):** Kato's classes are the primary input; the Rubin–Kolyvagin machine converts Euler systems to Selmer group bounds.
- **Group F (BSD Formula):** The Fitting ideal Main Conjecture is equivalent to the $p$-adic BSD formula.
- **Group D (Selmer Groups):** The Selmer group structure (cyclicity, number of generators) determines whether Fitting equals characteristic.
