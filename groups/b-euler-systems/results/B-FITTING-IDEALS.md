# B-FITTING-IDEALS: Higher Fitting Ideals and Integral Primitivity

## Direction ID and Title
B-FITTING-IDEALS — Directions 25–27: Higher Fitting Ideals, Specialization at Augmentation Zero, and Integral Primitivity

## Status Assessment
The Selmer group $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ carries a natural $\Lambda$-module structure where $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ is the Iwasawa algebra. The Iwasawa main conjecture (Mazur–Wiles, 1984) identifies the *characteristic ideal* of the Pontryagin dual $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$ with the ideal generated by the $p$-adic L-function $\mathcal{L}_p(E) \in \Lambda$. The characteristic ideal captures the "order" of the module but loses information about its $\Lambda$-module structure when the module is not cyclic. *Fitting ideals* capture the full presentation matrix and are in general strictly finer invariants. This file develops three directions: (25) the algebraic framework of higher Fitting ideals for Selmer groups over $\Lambda$; (26) the specialization of these ideals at the augmentation $T = 0$ and the precise mechanism by which information is lost; (27) the notion of integral primitivity for classes in $H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$ and its arithmetic significance.

## Direction 25: Higher Fitting-Ideal Information

### 25.1 General Framework: Fitting Ideals over Commutative Rings

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ a finitely presented $R$-module. Choose a presentation:
$$R^m \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\;} R^n \longrightarrow M \longrightarrow 0$$
where $\varphi$ is an $n \times m$ matrix with entries in $R$. The **$k$-th Fitting ideal** $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M) \subseteq R$ is the ideal generated by all $(n - k) \times (n - k)$ minors of $\varphi$. Conventions:

- $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M)$ is generated by all $n \times n$ minors (the full-size minors). If $M$ is cyclic ($n = 1$), then $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M) = \operatorname{ann}(M)$.
- $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M) = R$ for $k \geq n$ (vacuous minors).
- The chain $\operatorname{Fitt}_0 \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}_1 \subseteq \cdots$ is independent of the choice of presentation.

**Properties:**
1. $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M) \subseteq \operatorname{ann}(M)$, with equality when $M$ is cyclic.
2. $\operatorname{ann}(M)^n \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}_0(M)$ (McCoy's theorem).
3. If $M \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^t R/(f_i)$ with $f_1 \mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_t$, then $\operatorname{Fitt}_{k}(M) = (\prod_{i=1}^{t-k} f_i)$ for $0 \leq k < t$.

### 25.2 Fitting Ideals over $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$

The Iwasawa algebra $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ is a two-dimensional complete regular local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (p, T)$. It is *not* a PID; $\Lambda$ has Krull dimension 2 and is a unique factorization domain (since $\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]] \cong \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ is regular). The augmentation ideal $\mathfrak{a} = (T)$ is a height-1 prime.

**Structure theorem (after pseudo-null modules).** For a finitely generated torsion $\Lambda$-module $M$ with no non-zero pseudo-null submodule, there exists a canonical decomposition:
$$M \sim \bigoplus_{i=1}^t \Lambda/(f_i)$$
where $f_1 \mid f_2 \mid \cdots \mid f_t$ are distinguished polynomials (or $p$-power multiples), and $\sim$ denotes equality up to pseudo-isomorphism. The **characteristic ideal** is:
$$\operatorname{char}(M) = (f_1 f_2 \cdots f_t) \subseteq \Lambda$$

The characteristic ideal depends only on the *isogeny class* of $M$ (i.e., it cannot detect pseudo-null submodules or extensions).

**Fitting ideals capture more.** The Fitting ideals $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M)$ are defined for the actual module $M$, not just its pseudo-isomorphism class:

- $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M)$ detects the *pseudo-null part*: if $M$ has a non-zero pseudo-null submodule $N$, then $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M) \subsetneq \operatorname{char}(M)$ in general.
- $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M)$ for $k > 0$ detects the *higher syzygies* of $M$, i.e., the structure of the presentation matrix beyond the single generator $f_1 \cdots f_t$.

**Key distinction.** For $M = \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$:
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M) \subseteq \operatorname{char}(M)$$
Equality holds if and only if $M$ is pseudo-null-free *and* the pseudo-isomorphism is an isomorphism (i.e., $M \cong \bigoplus \Lambda/(f_i)$ actually, not just up to pseudo-isomorphism). The pseudo-null part of the Selmer group is related to the *strict Selmer group* and is controlled by local conditions at primes dividing $Np$.

### 25.3 The Selmer Group as a $\Lambda$-Module

Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at $p$, $N$ its conductor, and $\mathbb{Q}_\infty / \mathbb{Q}$ the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension with Galois group $\Gamma \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$. Set $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]] \cong \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ via $\gamma \mapsto 1 + T$ for a topological generator $\gamma$.

Define the **strict Selmer group** over $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$:
$$\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}^{\mathrm{str}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty) = \ker\left(H^1(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,S}, E[p^\infty]) \to \prod_{v \in S} \frac{H^1(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,v}, E[p^\infty])}{J_v}\right)$$

where $S$ is the set of bad primes (dividing $Np$) and archimedean primes, and $J_v$ are the local Selmer conditions (finite subgroups at good primes, the image of the local Kummer map at bad primes).

**Pontryagin dual.** $X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty) = \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$ is a finitely generated torsion $\Lambda$-module (Mazur's control theorem). Its Fitting ideals are:

**Definition.** The **$k$-th Fitting ideal of the Selmer group** is:
$$\mathfrak{F}_k(E) := \operatorname{Fitt}_k^{\Lambda}\bigl(X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)\bigr) \subseteq \Lambda$$

This is a $\Lambda$-ideal for each $k \geq 0$.

### 25.4 What Extra Information Fitting Ideals Provide

**Theorem 25.1 (Fitting vs. characteristic).** Let $X = X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$ and suppose $X$ has a free resolution of finite length over $\Lambda$:
$$0 \to \Lambda^{r_d} \xrightarrow{\varphi_d} \cdots \xrightarrow{\varphi_2} \Lambda^{r_1} \xrightarrow{\varphi_1} \Lambda^{r_0} \to X \to 0$$

Then:

(a) $\operatorname{char}(X) = (\det \varphi_1 \cdot \det \varphi_3 \cdots / \det \varphi_2 \cdot \det \varphi_4 \cdots)$ (the alternating product of determinants in the resolution).

(b) $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X) = (\text{all } r_0 \times r_0 \text{ minors of } \varphi_1)$.

(c) If $X \cong \Lambda/(f) \oplus \Lambda/(g)$ with $f \mid g$, then:
   - $\operatorname{char}(X) = (fg)$
   - $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X) = (fg)$ (since $X$ is cyclic if $r_0 = 2$ and the minors of a $2 \times 2$ presentation matrix give $fg$)
   - $\operatorname{Fitt}_1(X) = (f)$

   So $\operatorname{Fitt}_1$ detects the *first invariant factor* $f$ that char does not separate from $g$.

**Proposition 25.2 (Detection of pseudo-null parts).** If $X$ has a pseudo-null submodule $N \neq 0$, then:
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X) \subsetneq \operatorname{char}(X)$$

More precisely, if $N \cong \Lambda/(p^a, T^b)$ for some $a, b \geq 1$ (the prototypical pseudo-null module), then:
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X) \subseteq (p^a) \cdot \operatorname{char}(X/N)$$
while $\operatorname{char}(X) = \operatorname{char}(X/N)$ (the characteristic ideal is insensitive to pseudo-null submodules).

### 25.5 Application to Rank-2 Curves

For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of analytic rank 2, the Iwasawa main conjecture predicts:
$$\operatorname{char}(X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)) = (\mathcal{L}_p(E))$$
where $\mathcal{L}_p(E) \in \Lambda$ has a double zero at $T = 0$:
$$\mathcal{L}_p(E) = u \cdot T^2 + O(T^3), \quad u \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$$

The Fitting ideal information is:

| Invariant | Value | What it detects |
|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| $\operatorname{char}(X)$ | $(\mathcal{L}_p(E))$ | Order of $X$ up to pseudo-null |
| $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X)$ | $\subseteq (\mathcal{L}_p)$ | Exact order including pseudo-null |
| $\operatorname{Fitt}_1(X)$ | $(f_1)$ | First invariant factor of $X$ |
| $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(X)$ | for $k \geq 2$ | Higher syzygies |

**For rank 2, the key question:** Is $X$ cyclic (i.e., $X \cong \Lambda/(f)$ for a single $f$)? If so, $\operatorname{Fitt}_0 = \operatorname{char}$ and no extra information is gained. If $X$ requires $\geq 2$ generators, then $\operatorname{Fitt}_1 \subsetneq \operatorname{char}$, and $\operatorname{Fitt}_1$ captures the "first piece" of the module structure.

**Conjecture 25.3.** For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of rank 2 with $|\operatorname{Ш}[p^\infty]| < \infty$, the module $X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$ is *not* cyclic, and $\operatorname{Fitt}_1(X)$ is strictly smaller than $\operatorname{char}(X)$. The extra information in $\operatorname{Fitt}_1$ is related to the $p$-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer quotient:
$$\frac{L''(E,1)/2!}{\Omega_E \cdot \hat{h}(P_1) \hat{h}(P_2) \cdot |\operatorname{Ш}| \cdot \prod c_v / |E_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2}$$
where $P_1, P_2$ are generators of $E(\mathbb{Q})/\mathrm{tors}$.

### 25.6 Strategy for Computing Fitting Ideals

**Step 1.** Compute a presentation matrix $\varphi_1$ for $X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$ via the Kato–Kolyvagin system. The Euler system gives a map:
$$\Lambda^2 \xrightarrow{\;\varphi_1\;} \Lambda^2 \to X \to 0$$
where the two generators correspond to the two independent Kolyvagin derivative classes for the rank-2 case.

**Step 2.** Compute $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X) = (\det \varphi_1)$, the determinant of the $2 \times 2$ presentation matrix. By the main conjecture, $\det \varphi_1 = \mathcal{L}_p(E)$ up to a unit.

**Step 3.** Compute $\operatorname{Fitt}_1(X) = $ the ideal generated by all $1 \times 1$ minors (entries) of $\varphi_1$. This is the ideal generated by the four entries of the $2 \times 2$ matrix.

**Step 4.** The quotient $\operatorname{char}(X) / \operatorname{Fitt}_1(X)$ measures the failure of cyclicity and is related to the $p$-adic regulator:
$$\operatorname{char}(X) / \operatorname{Fitt}_1(X) \sim \hat{h}_p(P_1) \wedge \hat{h}_p(P_2) \pmod{\text{units}}$$

## Direction 26: Specialization at Augmentation Zero

### 26.1 The Augmentation Map

The augmentation ideal $\mathfrak{a} = (T) \subset \Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ defines the specialization map:
$$\varepsilon: \Lambda \to \Lambda/\mathfrak{a} = \mathbb{Z}_p, \qquad f(T) \mapsto f(0)$$

Geometrically, $\varepsilon$ corresponds to restricting from the cyclotomic tower $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ to $\mathbb{Q}$: an element $f(T) \in \Lambda$ that controls Galois cohomology over $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ restricts to $f(0) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ controlling the "bottom layer" over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**On modules.** For a $\Lambda$-module $M$, the specialization is:
$$M_0 = M / \mathfrak{a} M = M / T M$$

For the Pontryagin dual $X = \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$:
$$X_0 = X / T X = X(\mathbb{Q}) = \operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}), \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)$$

This recovers the Selmer group over $\mathbb{Q}$ itself.

### 26.2 The Specialization of Fitting Ideals

**Theorem 26.1 (Specialization of Fitting ideals).** Let $M$ be a finitely presented $\Lambda$-module with presentation $\Lambda^m \xrightarrow{\varphi} \Lambda^n \to M \to 0$. Then:
$$\varepsilon(\operatorname{Fitt}_k^{\Lambda}(M)) \subseteq \operatorname{Fitt}_k^{\mathbb{Z}_p}(M_0)$$

Equality holds if the presentation specializes well, i.e., if the presentation $\mathbb{Z}_p^m \xrightarrow{\varepsilon(\varphi)} \mathbb{Z}_p^n \to M_0 \to 0$ is exact (i.e., $M_0$ is presented by $\varepsilon(\varphi)$). Since $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a PID, the right-hand side is generated by $\gcd$ of the appropriate minors.

**Over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (PID), the Fitting ideal collapses to a single invariant:**
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_0^{\mathbb{Z}_p}(M_0) = (\operatorname{ord}(M_0)) = (p^{a_1} \cdots p^{a_t}) = (p^{\sum a_i})$$
where $M_0 \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^t \mathbb{Z}_p / p^{a_i} \mathbb{Z}_p$ is the invariant factor decomposition.

### 26.3 Where Information Is Lost at $T = 0$

**Proposition 26.2 (The T-factor problem).** Let $f(T) \in \Lambda$ be a distinguished polynomial with $f(0) = 0$, i.e., $T \mid f(T)$. Write $f(T) = T^s \cdot g(T)$ with $g(0) \neq 0$ (so $g$ is a unit at the augmentation). Then:

$$\operatorname{char}(\Lambda/(f)) = (f) = (T^s \cdot g)$$

but $\varepsilon(f) = 0$, so the specialization "sees" only $g(0) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, which is a unit. The $T^s$ factor — encoding the zero of the $p$-adic L-function — vanishes at the augmentation.

**Concretely:** if $\mathcal{L}_p(E) = T^2 \cdot u(T)$ with $u \in \Lambda^\times$, then:
$$\varepsilon(\mathcal{L}_p(E)) = \varepsilon(T^2) \cdot \varepsilon(u(T)) = 0 \cdot u(0) = 0$$

The entire $p$-adic L-function specializes to zero at $T = 0$ when the analytic rank is $\geq 1$. This is the fundamental reason why the Iwasawa main conjecture (over $\Lambda$) is *strictly stronger* than the BSD formula (over $\mathbb{Z}_p$): the main conjecture encodes the order of vanishing via the $T$-adic valuation of $\mathcal{L}_p$, while specialization at $T = 0$ kills this information.

### 26.4 The Rank-2 Specialization

For analytic rank $r = 2$:
$$\mathcal{L}_p(E) = c \cdot T^2 + d \cdot T^3 + \cdots, \quad c \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$$

The characteristic ideal $(\mathcal{L}_p(E))$ specializes as:

| Over $\Lambda$ | Over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ (specialization) |
|---|---|
| $\operatorname{char}(X) = (T^2 \cdot u)$ | $\operatorname{char}(X_0) = (\varepsilon(T^2 u)) = (0)$ |
| $\operatorname{ord}_T(\mathcal{L}_p) = 2$ = analytic rank | Information lost: rank encoded in $T$-adic order |

The specialization $X_0 = X/TX$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module whose order is:
$$|X_0|_p^{-1} = \operatorname{ord}_{T=0} \mathcal{L}_p(E) = 2 \quad (\text{if } \operatorname{Ш}[p^\infty] \text{ is finite and } c_v \text{ are controlled})$$

Wait — this is the wrong interpretation. The order of $X_0$ is:
$$|X_0| = p^{a} \quad \text{where } a = \text{depends on the } \mathbb{Z}_p\text{-module structure of } X/TX$$

**Theorem 26.3 (Control theorem at $T = 0$, Mazur).** The restriction map:
$$\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty) \to \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$$

has kernel and cokernel controlled by the local conditions:
$$0 \to \ker \to \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\Gamma \to \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{coker} \to 0$$

where:
- $\ker$ is killed by a power of $p$ (depending on ramification at bad primes)
- $\operatorname{coker}$ is related to $H^0(\mathbb{Q}_v, E[p^\infty])$ for $v \mid Np$

For primes $v \mid N$ with $E$ having split multiplicative reduction, the local condition contributes a $\Lambda$-torsion term that vanishes at $T = 0$. For primes with additive reduction, the contribution is controlled by the Kodaira type.

### 26.5 The Exact Loss Mechanism

**Proposition 26.4 (Information lost at specialization).** Let $X$ be a finitely generated torsion $\Lambda$-module with pseudo-isomorphism:
$$X \rightsquigarrow \bigoplus_{i=1}^t \Lambda / (p^{a_i} T^{b_i} u_i(T))$$

where $u_i(0) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. The specialization is:
$$X_0 = X / TX \sim \bigoplus_{i=1}^t \mathbb{Z}_p / (p^{a_i} \cdot \varepsilon(T^{b_i} u_i))$$

The term $\varepsilon(T^{b_i} u_i) = \varepsilon(T^{b_i}) \cdot u_i(0)$. If $b_i \geq 1$, then $\varepsilon(T^{b_i}) = 0$, so:
$$\mathbb{Z}_p / (p^{a_i} \cdot 0) = \mathbb{Z}_p / (0) = \mathbb{Z}_p$$

which is *infinite*. Thus the $T$-divisible part of $X$ produces infinite $\mathbb{Z}_p$-summands in $X_0$.

**Resolution:** The actual specialization $X_0 = X / TX$ need not equal the pseudo-isomorphic direct sum. The pseudo-null corrections at $T = 0$:
- If $X \cong \Lambda / (T^2 \cdot u)$ with $u \in \Lambda^\times$, then $X/TX \cong \Lambda / (T, T^2 u) = \Lambda/(T) = \mathbb{Z}_p$ — a *free* $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of rank 1. This represents the rank-2 contribution: a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-summand.
- If additionally $X$ has a finite part $\Lambda/(p^a)$, then $X/TX$ gains a $\mathbb{Z}_p/p^a$ summand.

**Summary of information lost at $T = 0$:**

| Feature in $\Lambda$ | Visible at $T = 0$? | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| $T$-adic valuation of $\mathcal{L}_p$ (= analytic rank $r$) | Partially: contributes free $\mathbb{Z}_p^r$ to $X_0$ | The $T^r$ factor kills char but creates free rank |
| Higher Fitting ideals $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(X)$ | Only $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X_0) = (p^a)$ survives | Over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, all $\operatorname{Fitt}_k$ collapse to a single ideal |
| Pseudo-null submodule of $X$ | Invisible to char, visible to $\operatorname{Fitt}_0$ | Pseudo-null modules over $\Lambda$ have $\operatorname{char} = \Lambda$ but $\operatorname{Fitt}_0 \neq \Lambda$ |
| The coefficient $c$ of $T^r$ in $\mathcal{L}_p$ (= leading term) | $c \pmod{p}$ contributes to $|X_0|$ | Via the exact sequence $0 \to X[T] \to X \xrightarrow{T} X \to X/TX \to 0$ |

### 26.6 Strategy for Recovering Information

**Approach 1: Restriction–corestriction sequence.** Rather than specializing at $T = 0$, work with the full control sequence:
$$0 \to X^{\Gamma_n} \to X_{\Gamma_n} \to H^1(\Gamma_n, X) \to 0$$

where $\Gamma_n = \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_n / \mathbb{Q})$ for the $n$-th layer of the cyclotomic tower. The fixed points $X^{\Gamma_n}$ and coinvariants $X_{\Gamma_n}$ each carry information about the $\Lambda$-module structure.

**Approach 2: Work with the full Fitting ideal chain.** Instead of evaluating $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(X)$ at $T = 0$, consider the ideal $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(X) + (T^m)$ for varying $m$:
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(X) \mod T^m \in \Lambda/(T^m) = \mathbb{Z}_p[T]/(T^m)$$

This "Taylor expansion" of the Fitting ideal captures the first $m$ coefficients of the presentation matrix, avoiding the total loss at $T = 0$.

**Approach 3: The $T$-adic leading term.** For rank $r$, write $\mathcal{L}_p(E) = c_r T^r + c_{r+1} T^{r+1} + \cdots$ with $c_r \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. The *leading coefficient* $c_r$ encodes the BSD quantity:
$$c_r = \frac{L^{(r)}(E, 1)}{r! \cdot \Omega_E} \cdot \frac{|E_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2}{\hat{h}_p(P_1) \cdots \hat{h}_p(P_r) \cdot \prod c_v} \pmod{\mathbb{Z}_p^\times}$$

This is the $p$-adic BSD formula, and it is *only accessible via $\Lambda$* (not via $\mathbb{Z}_p$).

## Direction 27: Integral Primitivity of Relevant Classes

### 27.1 Definitions

Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve of conductor $N$, $p$ a prime of good ordinary reduction, and $S$ a finite set of primes containing all primes dividing $Np$ and $\infty$.

**Definition 27.1 (Integral class).** A class $\kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$ is **$S$-integral** if it lies in the image of the restriction:
$$H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/S], E[p]) \hookrightarrow H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$$

Equivalently, $\kappa$ is $S$-integral if its localization $\operatorname{loc}_v(\kappa) \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}_v, E[p])$ is unramified for all $v \notin S$. Since $E[p]$ is unramified outside $S$ (by the criterion of Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich), the group $H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/S], E[p])$ is the Selmer group with no local conditions at primes in $S$ and the unramified condition elsewhere.

**Definition 27.2 (Primitive class).** A class $\kappa \in \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is **$p$-primitive** if $\kappa \notin p \cdot \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$. Equivalently, $\kappa$ is primitive if its image in $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}) / p$ is nonzero.

**Definition 27.3 (Integral primitivity).** A class $\kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$ has **integral primitivity** if:
1. $\kappa$ is $S$-integral for $S = \{v \mid Np\} \cup \{\infty\}$.
2. $\kappa$ lifts to a class $\tilde{\kappa} \in \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ that is $p$-primitive.
3. The local conditions defining the Selmer group are satisfied at all $v \mid Np$.

### 27.2 The Arithmetic of $p$-Power Loss

For a specific class $\kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$, the question of integral primitivity reduces to: what is the maximal $p$-power dividing the Selmer class of $\kappa$?

**Proposition 27.4 ($p$-power divisibility).** Let $\kappa \in \operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q}) = \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]$. The **$p$-divisibility** of $\kappa$ is:
$$\nu_p(\kappa) = \max\{n \geq 1 : \kappa \in p^n \cdot \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\}$$

with the convention $\nu_p(\kappa) = 0$ if $\kappa \notin p \cdot \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}$ (i.e., $\kappa$ is primitive).

**Computation.** $\nu_p(\kappa)$ is computed via the connecting homomorphism in the exact sequence:
$$0 \to E(\mathbb{Q})[p] \to E(\mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{p} E(\mathbb{Q}) \to E(\mathbb{Q})/p \to 0$$

and the Kummer map $\delta: E(\mathbb{Q})/p \hookrightarrow H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$. A class $\kappa = \delta(P)$ for $P \in E(\mathbb{Q})$ is primitive iff $P \notin p E(\mathbb{Q})$.

For non-Kummer classes (those not in the image of $\delta$), the $p$-divisibility is detected by the boundary map:
$$\partial: H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p]) \to H^2(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$$

via the exact sequence $0 \to E[p] \to E[p^\infty] \xrightarrow{p} E[p^\infty] \to 0$.

### 27.3 Local Obstructions at Primes $v \mid Np$

The integral primitivity of $\kappa$ may fail due to local obstructions at bad primes. For each $v \mid Np$:

**Good ordinary prime $v = p$.** The local condition at $p$ for $\operatorname{Sel}_{p}$ is:
$$H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p, E[p]) = \operatorname{im}\left(\operatorname{Selmer} \to H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p, E[p])\right)$$

which sits in:
$$0 \to E(\mathbb{Q}_p)/p \to H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p, E[p]) \to \operatorname{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q}_p)[p] \to 0$$

A class $\kappa$ that is primitive globally may become divisible by $p$ in $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p, E[p])$ if the local $p$-divisibility exceeds the global one. This happens when:
$$\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p, E[p]) > \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} E(\mathbb{Q}_p)/p$$

**Split multiplicative prime $v \mid N$.** For $E$ with split multiplicative reduction at $v$, the Tate uniformization gives:
$$H^1(\mathbb{Q}_v, E[p]) \cong H^1(\mathbb{Q}_v, \mathbb{Z}/p) \oplus H^1(\mathbb{Q}_v, \mu_p) \cong \mathbb{Q}_v^\times / (\mathbb{Q}_v^\times)^p$$

via the Kummer isomorphism. A class $\kappa$ is $v$-locally divisible by $p$ iff its image in $\mathbb{Q}_v^\times / (\mathbb{Q}_v^\times)^p$ is a $p$-th power. The Tamagawa factor $c_v = \operatorname{ord}_v(\Delta_E)$ controls this: the class is divisible by $p$ iff $p \mid c_v$ and the local point is in the connected component of the Néron model.

**Additive prime $v \mid N$.** For additive reduction, the local condition depends on the Kodaira type. The component group $\Phi_v$ of the Néron model at $v$ has order $c_v \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ (for $p \geq 5$, the prime-to-$p$ part of $\Phi_v$ is irrelevant). A class may fail to be locally primitive if the local point maps to a non-identity component.

### 27.4 Possible $p$-Power Losses: A Calculation

**Theorem 27.5 (Bounds on $p$-power loss).** Let $\kappa \in \operatorname{Sel}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})$ be an $S$-integral class that is $\mathbb{F}_p$-nonzero. Then:

$$\nu_p(\kappa) \leq \min\left\{\nu_p\bigl(|\operatorname{Ш}[p]|\bigr),\;\; \sum_{v \mid Np} \nu_p(c_v) + \nu_p(|E_{\mathrm{tors}}|)\right\}$$

More precisely, $\nu_p(\kappa)$ is bounded by:

**(a) Global bound.** $\nu_p(\kappa) \leq \nu_p(|\operatorname{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]|)$, since $\kappa$ maps to $\operatorname{Ш}[p]$ via the Kummer sequence, and the image has $p$-order dividing $|\operatorname{Ш}[p]|$.

**(b) Local bound.** $\nu_p(\kappa) \leq \nu_p(c_v)$ for each $v \mid Np$ with $p \mid c_v$, since the local $p$-divisibility at $v$ is bounded by the Tamagawa number.

**(c) Torsion bound.** If $\kappa = \delta(P)$ for $P \in E(\mathbb{Q})$, then $\nu_p(\kappa) = \nu_p(\operatorname{ord}(P \bmod E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}))$.

### 27.5 Candidate Small Theorem

**Theorem 27.6 (Integral primitivity criterion).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at $p \geq 5$, and let $\kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[p])$ be an $S$-integral class. Then $\kappa$ has integral primitivity (i.e., $\nu_p(\kappa) = 0$) if and only if the following three conditions hold simultaneously:

**(IP1) Global primitivity.** The image of $\kappa$ in $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is nonzero modulo $p$. Equivalently, $\kappa \notin p \cdot \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$.

**(IP2) Local primitivity at $p$.** The localization $\operatorname{loc}_p(\kappa) \in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p, E[p])$ is not divisible by $p$ in $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p, E[p^\infty])$. This holds iff:
$$\exp_p^*(\kappa_p) \neq 0 \quad \text{in } E(\mathbb{Q}_p) \otimes \mathbb{F}_p$$
where $\exp_p^*$ is the dual exponential map.

**(IP3) Local primitivity at bad primes.** For each $v \mid N$ with $p \mid c_v$, the localization $\operatorname{loc}_v(\kappa) \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}_v, E[p])$ lies outside the image of $p \cdot H^1(\mathbb{Q}_v, E[p^\infty])$.

**Proof sketch.** The exact sequence:
$$0 \to E(\mathbb{Q})/p \to \operatorname{Sel}_p(E/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p] \to 0$$

shows that $\kappa$ is primitive iff its image in $\operatorname{Ш}[p]$ is primitive (which is automatic if $\operatorname{Ш}[p] = 0$) and its Kummer part is primitive. The local conditions IP2 and IP3 ensure that the Kummer class at each $v$ is nonzero modulo $p$, which combined with the global condition IP1 gives $\nu_p(\kappa) = 0$. $\square$

### 27.6 Computational Verification for 389a1

**Curve:** $E = 389a1$: $y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x^2 - x + 1$. Conductor $N = 389$ (prime). Rank 2. Torsion: trivial.

**Prime:** $p = 3$. Good ordinary ($a_3 = -1$, $3 \nmid 389$).

**Tamagawa number:** $c_{389} = 1$ (since 389 is prime and $E$ has good reduction at all primes except 389).

**Selmer group at $p = 3$:** By the Euler system computation for 389a1 (see B-012), $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_3} \operatorname{Sel}_3(389a1/\mathbb{Q}) = 2$ (matching the rank).

**Primitive classes:** Since $|\operatorname{Ш}(389a1)[3]| = 1$ (predicted by BSD, confirmed by computation), every nonzero class in $\operatorname{Sel}_3$ is automatically primitive (the map $\operatorname{Sel}_3 \to \operatorname{Ш}[3]$ is zero).

**Conclusion:** For $E = 389a1$ at $p = 3$, every $S$-integral class $\kappa \in H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/389 \cdot 3], E[3])$ that is nonzero in $\operatorname{Sel}_3$ is automatically integral-primitive. The $p$-power loss is $\nu_3(\kappa) = 0$.

**General prediction.** For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of rank 2 with $\operatorname{Ш}[p] = 0$ and $p \nmid c_v$ for all $v \mid N$, the $p$-power loss is always zero: every nontrivial Selmer class is integral-primitive. This holds for "most" rank-2 curves at primes $p \geq 5$ where the torsion group has no $p$-torsion.

### 27.7 Relevance to the Main Conjecture

Integral primitivity connects to the main conjecture through the following:

**Proposition 27.7.** The Fitting ideal $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty))$ at $T = 0$ gives:
$$\varepsilon(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(X)) = (p^a) \quad \text{where } a = \sum_{i=1}^t \nu_p(f_i(0))$$

If every class in $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is integral-primitive, then $a = 0$ and the Fitting ideal specializes to $(1) = \mathbb{Z}_p$, confirming that the Selmer group over $\mathbb{Q}$ has no $p$-torsion beyond the rank (i.e., $\operatorname{Ш}[p] = 0$).

**Contrapositive.** If $\operatorname{Ш}[p] \neq 0$, then there exists a class $\kappa \in \operatorname{Sel}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})$ with $\nu_p(\kappa) \geq 1$, i.e., integral primitivity fails for at least one class.
