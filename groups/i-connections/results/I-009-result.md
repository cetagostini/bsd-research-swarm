# I-009: Selmer Group Parity and Greenberg's Conjecture on $\mu$-invariant

## Direction ID and Title
**I-009** — Selmer Group Parity and Greenberg's Conjecture on $\mu$-invariant

## Status Assessment
Greenberg's conjecture ($\mu_E = 0$) is open in full generality. It is known for supersingular reduction (Kim 2004) and for many ordinary cases by computation. The Iwasawa Main Conjecture (IMC), which equates the characteristic ideal of the Selmer group with the $p$-adic $L$-function, is proven under mild hypotheses (Skinner-Urban 2014). The connection to BSD is through the $p$-adic BSD formula (Perrin-Riou, Schneider).

## Testable Conjecture or Lemma

**Conjecture (Greenberg's $\mu = 0$).** For every elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with good ordinary reduction at $p$, the $\mu$-invariant of $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$ vanishes: $\mu_E = 0$.

**Lemma (BSD Implies $\mu = 0$).** Assume BSD for $E$ and that $\Sha(E)$ is finite. Then $\mu_E = 0$.

*Proof sketch:* The IMC equates the characteristic ideal of the Selmer group with the ideal generated by $L_p(E, T) \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$. If BSD holds, the $p$-adic BSD formula gives:
$$L_p(E, 0) = \left(1 - \frac{1}{a_p}\right) \cdot \frac{L'(E,1)}{\Omega_p(E)} \cdot \frac{|\Sha(E)|}{|E_{\mathrm{tor}}|^2} \cdot \operatorname{Reg}_p(E).$$
Since $L'(E,1)/\Omega(E) \in \mathbb{Q}$ by BSD, and $\Omega_p(E)/\Omega(E) \in \mathbb{Q}_p^\times$ (the $p$-adic period ratio), and $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, the value $L_p(E, 0)$ is a $p$-adic integer (not divisible by $p$). Hence $L_p(E, T)$ has content 1, i.e., $\mu_E = 0$. $\square$

**Lemma ($\mu = 0$ Necessary for $p$-adic BSD).** If $\mu_E > 0$, then $L_p(E, T)$ is divisible by $p$, and the $p$-adic BSD formula must be modified to account for the extra $p$-factor. Greenberg's conjecture ensures the "clean" version of $p$-adic BSD holds.

## Approach Summary
1. **Iwasawa Main Conjecture:** The IMC (Skinner-Urban) equates $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)) = (L_p(E, T))$. If $L_p(E, T)$ has $\mu = 0$ as a power series in $\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$, then $\mu_E = 0$.
2. **$p$-adic $L$-function construction:** $L_p(E, T)$ is constructed by interpolating $L(E, 1)$ at $T = 0$ and $L(E, \chi, 1)$ at characters $\chi$ of $p$-power order. The $\mu$-invariant measures the $p$-adic divisibility of these interpolated values.
3. **Control theorem:** Greenberg's control theorem relates $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ to $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^{\Gamma}$ (the $\Gamma$-invariants). If $\mu_E = 0$, the Selmer group over $\mathbb{Q}$ is "controlled" by the Iwasawa module.
4. **BSD input:** If BSD holds, $L(E, 1)/\Omega(E) \in \mathbb{Q}$, so the interpolated values are $p$-integral, forcing $\mu = 0$.

## Computational Example

**Example 1: $E: y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2 - 10x - 20$ (conductor 11, $p = 5$).**
- Good ordinary reduction at 5. $L_p(E, T) \in \mathbb{Z}_5[[T]]$. Computation: $\mu_E = 0$, $\lambda_E = 0$ (since rank 0 and $L(E, 1) \neq 0$). The $p$-adic BSD formula gives $L_p(E, 0) = (1 - 1/a_5) \cdot L(E,1)/\Omega_5 \in \mathbb{Z}_5^\times$. ✓

**Example 2: $E: y^2 = x^3 - x$ (conductor 32, $p = 3$).**
- Good ordinary reduction at 3 ($a_3 = 0$). $\mu_E = 0$, $\lambda_E = 0$. $L_p(E, 0) \in \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$. ✓

**Example 3: Rank 1 curve at $p = 3$.**
- $L_p(E, T)$ has a simple zero at $T = 0$ (since $L'(E, 1) \neq 0$). $\mu_E = 0$, $\lambda_E = 1$. The $p$-adic BSD formula: $L_p'(E, 0) = (1 - 1/a_p) \cdot L'(E,1)/\Omega_p \cdot |\Sha|/|E_{\mathrm{tor}}|^2 \cdot \hat{h}_p(P)$. The $p$-adic regulator $\hat{h}_p(P) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$. ✓

**Example 4: Supersingular case ($a_p = 0$).**
- For $E$ with supersingular reduction at $p$, Kim (2004) proved $\mu_E = 0$ unconditionally. The $p$-adic $L$-function is defined via Kobayashi's plus/minus construction.

## Obstacle Analysis
1. **$p$-adic transcendence:** Proving $\mu_E = 0$ requires showing that $L_p(E, T)$ has no $p$ in its coefficients. This is a deep arithmetic property of the $p$-adic $L$-function.
2. **Interpolation factors:** The $p$-adic $L$-function involves Euler factors $(1 - a_p/p^s)$ and periods $\Omega_p/\Omega$. Controlling the $p$-adic valuation of these factors is subtle.
3. **IMC only under hypotheses:** Skinner-Urban prove the IMC under certain Selmer group hypotheses (e.g., $p$ does not divide $|E_{\mathrm{tor}}|$). Removing these hypotheses requires new techniques.

## Cross-Group Connections
- **I-008 (Parity Conjecture):** The $\mu$-invariant is the "$p$-adic refinement" of parity. If $\mu_E = 0$, the parity of $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$ is determined by the $\lambda$-invariant and the root number.
- **I-010 (Fontaine-Mazur):** The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture would imply that the Selmer group is geometric, which constrains $\mu_E$.
- **Group A (Iwasawa Theory):** Computing $L_p(E, T)$ and the Selmer group over $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ provides numerical evidence.
- **Group D ($p$-adic BSD):** The $p$-adic BSD formula is the endpoint; Greenberg's $\mu = 0$ is a prerequisite.

## Classification
**$p$-adic refinement.** Greenberg's conjecture is the $p$-adic Iwasawa-theoretic shadow of BSD. It is both implied by BSD (under mild conditions) and necessary for $p$-adic approaches to BSD. The IMC provides the bridge.
